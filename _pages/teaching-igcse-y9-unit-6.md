Statistics is only as good as the data behind it. This unit follows the whole journey: **plan** a survey, **collect** unbiased data, then **summarise and compare** it with averages, range and graphs.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>6.1–6.2 Planning a survey and collecting data</h4>

- **Primary data** you collect yourself; **secondary data** was collected by someone else.
- A **sample** should be large enough and **random** — every member of the population has an equal chance of being chosen. A sample that leaves groups out is **biased**.
- A good survey question is clear, is **not leading**, states a time period, and has response boxes that **do not overlap** and cover every possible answer.
- Choose a sensible degree of **accuracy** for measurements, and group continuous data in equal classes such as $20\lt t\le30$.
- A **two-way table** shows two categories at once; every row and column must add to its total.

<h4>6.3 Averages and range</h4>

- **Mean** uses every value but is distorted by an **outlier**; the **median** is not.
- For grouped data: the **modal class** has the highest frequency, and

  estimated mean $=\dfrac{\text{total of (midpoint}\times\text{frequency)}}{\text{total frequency}}$.

- It is an *estimate* because the exact values inside each class are unknown. Estimated range $=$ largest possible value $-$ smallest possible value.

<h4>6.4 Displaying and analysing data</h4>

- A **frequency polygon** joins the points (midpoint, frequency) with straight lines; two polygons on the same axes make comparison easy.
- A **scatter graph** shows **positive**, **negative** or **no correlation**. A **line of best fit** follows the trend with roughly equal numbers of points on each side.
- Estimating *inside* the range of the data is reliable; estimating *outside* it (extrapolation) is not.
- To **compare** two sets of data, quote one average and one measure of spread, in context.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> The heights of 20 plants are grouped: $0\lt h\le10$ (frequency 3), $10\lt h\le20$ (frequency 9), $20\lt h\le30$ (frequency 8). Estimate the mean height.

Midpoints 5, 15, 25. Total $=5\times3+15\times9+25\times8=15+135+200=350$. Estimated mean $=\dfrac{350}{20}=17.5$ cm.
</div>

**Common slips:** dividing by the number of classes instead of the total frequency · using class end points instead of midpoints · plotting a frequency polygon at the ends of the classes · comparing two data sets without mentioning spread · response boxes such as 0–2 and 2–4 that overlap.
