Organising data in tables, summarising it with **averages and the range**, and showing it in the standard diagrams — pie charts, stem-and-leaf, two-way tables, scatter graphs. The skill is choosing the right summary and reading it honestly.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>10.1 Tables and averages</h4>

- **Mean** $=\dfrac{\text{total of the values}}{\text{how many}}$. From a frequency table: multiply each value by its frequency, add, divide by the total frequency.
- **Median** = middle value when the data is in order (with $n$ values, the $\frac{n+1}{2}$th). **Mode** = most common value. **Range** = largest − smallest (a measure of **spread**, not an average).
- **Grouped frequency table:** classes like $45\le m<50$; each value goes in exactly one class.
- **Two-way table:** rows and columns with totals; fill in the gaps by subtracting from the totals.

<h4>10.2 Stem and leaf diagrams</h4>

- The stem holds the first digit(s), the leaves the last digit, in order, with a **key** (10 | 7 = 10.7). Range, median and mode can be read straight off it.

<h4>10.3 Pie charts</h4>

- Each sector's angle is proportional to its frequency: angle $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}}\times360^\circ$. To read one: items $=\dfrac{\text{angle}}{360}\times$ total.
- Check the angles add to $360^\circ$; label every sector.

<h4>10.4 Comparing data</h4>

- Compare two sets with an **average** (which is typically bigger) **and** a measure of **spread** (which is more consistent). Say what the numbers mean in context: "the Petrou family usually spend more, but their spending varies more from week to week".
- The mean is pulled by extreme values; the median is not.

<h4>10.5 Scatter graphs and correlation</h4>

- One variable across, the other up, one point per item. **Positive correlation**: as one increases so does the other (points rise). **Negative**: one goes up as the other goes down. **No correlation**: scattered.
- A **line of best fit** passes through the middle of the points (roughly equal numbers each side) and is used to **estimate** one value from the other. Estimates beyond the data are unreliable.
- Correlation is not cause: ice-cream sales and sunburn both rise in summer.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Scores: 7, 3, 9, 7, 5, 11, 7, 2. Find the mean, median, mode and range.

Mean $=51\div8=6.4$ (1 d.p.). In order: 2, 3, 5, 7, 7, 7, 9, 11 → median is the mean of the 4th and 5th: 7. Mode 7. Range $11-2=9$.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> 40 students chose a sport: football 18, tennis 10, swimming 12. Find the pie-chart angles.

$360\div40=9^\circ$ per student: football $162^\circ$, tennis $90^\circ$, swimming $108^\circ$ (sum $360^\circ$ ✓).
</div>

**Common slips:** median without ordering the data first · mean from a frequency table by dividing by the number of *rows* · reading a pie chart as counts without the total · drawing a line of best fit through the origin or through the first and last points.
