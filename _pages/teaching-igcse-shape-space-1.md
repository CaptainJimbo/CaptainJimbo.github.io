<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>Angle facts (the reasons you must quote)</h4>

- Angles on a **straight line** add up to $180^\circ$; angles **around a point** add up to $360^\circ$; **vertically opposite** angles are equal.
- **Parallel lines** cut by a transversal: **alternate** angles (Z) are equal, **corresponding** angles (F) are equal, **co-interior** angles (C) add up to $180^\circ$.
- Mark every angle you find on a neat sketch — it's how you spot the next step.

<h4>Triangles</h4>

- Angle sum $180^\circ$. The **exterior angle** equals the sum of the two **opposite interior** angles.
- **Isosceles**: two equal sides, two equal base angles; the line of symmetry splits it into two congruent right-angled triangles. **Equilateral**: all $60^\circ$.

<h4>Quadrilaterals and polygons</h4>

- Interior angles of a quadrilateral sum to $360^\circ$ (it splits into two triangles).
- An $n$-sided polygon splits into $n-2$ triangles: **interior angle sum** $=(n-2)\times180^\circ$.
- The **exterior angles** of *any* polygon add up to $360^\circ$.
- **Regular** polygon: exterior angle $=\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$, interior angle $=180^\circ-\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. Pentagon $108^\circ$, hexagon $120^\circ$, octagon $135^\circ$.

<h4>Constructions (compasses and ruler only)</h4>

- **Perpendicular bisector** of $AB$: arcs of the same radius (more than half $AB$) from $A$ and from $B$, join the two crossings.
- **Angle bisector**: arc from the vertex cutting both arms, then equal arcs from those two points; join the vertex to the crossing.
- $60^\circ$: two equal arcs from the ends of a line segment (equilateral triangle). $90^\circ$: perpendicular bisector; $45^\circ$: bisect it. $30^\circ$: bisect $60^\circ$.

<h4>Similar triangles</h4>

- Two triangles are **similar** if their angles are equal; then corresponding sides are in the **same ratio** (the **scale factor**).
- A line **parallel** to one side of a triangle cuts off a smaller similar triangle (corresponding angles).
- To find a length: identify the pair of corresponding sides you know both of, find the scale factor, multiply.
- **Scale** $1:50\,000$ means 1 cm on the map is $50\,000$ cm $=500$ m in real life.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> $ABC$ and $CDE$ are straight lines, $AE\parallel BD$. Angle $BDE$ (at $D$, on the far side) is $99^\circ$ and angle $EAB=68^\circ$. Find angle $CBD$.

$\angle CDB=180-99=81^\circ$ (straight line). $\angle CBD=\angle CAE=68^\circ$ (corresponding angles, $BD\parallel AE$). So $\angle CBD=68^\circ$; and the third angle of triangle $BCD$ is $180-81-68=31^\circ$.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A regular polygon has interior angles of $162^\circ$. How many sides?

Exterior angle $=180-162=18^\circ$. Exterior angles sum to $360^\circ$, so $n=\dfrac{360}{18}=20$ sides.
</div>

**Common slips:** quoting "Z angles" without saying *which* pair · using $180^\circ$ for a quadrilateral · forgetting that the exterior-angle rule needs the *opposite* interior angles · mixing up which side of a similar triangle corresponds to which.
