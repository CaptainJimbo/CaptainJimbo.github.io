Area formulas for the shapes that appear everywhere, how to cut a complicated shape into simple ones, and then into three dimensions: **surface area**, **volume** and the units that go with them.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>4.1–4.2 Area of 2D shapes</h4>

- **Triangle:** $A=\tfrac{1}{2}\times\text{base}\times\text{height}$ — the height is the **perpendicular** height, not a sloping side.
- **Parallelogram:** $A=\text{base}\times\text{perpendicular height}$.
- **Trapezium:** $A=\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b)\,h$ — average of the two parallel sides, times the distance between them.
- **Compound shapes:** split into rectangles and triangles and add; or take a big shape and **subtract** the hole.
- Same units before you multiply: 35 mm $=3.5$ cm.

<h4>4.3 Properties of 3D solids</h4>

- **Faces** (flat surfaces), **edges** (where faces meet), **vertices** (corners). Cube: 6, 12, 8. Triangular prism: 5, 9, 6. Square-based pyramid: 5, 8, 5.
- A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way through.

<h4>4.4 Surface area</h4>

- Add up the areas of **all** the faces. A cuboid has three pairs of equal faces: $2(lw+lh+wh)$.
- An **open** box has no lid — leave that face out.

<h4>4.5 Volume</h4>

- **Cuboid:** $V=l\times w\times h$. **Prism:** $V=\text{area of cross-section}\times\text{length}$.
- Working backwards: if the volume and two lengths are known, divide to find the third.

<h4>4.6 Units of area and volume</h4>

- $1\ \text{cm}^2=100\ \text{mm}^2$, $\;1\ \text{m}^2=10\,000\ \text{cm}^2$ (square the length conversion).
- $1\ \text{cm}^3=1\,000\ \text{mm}^3$, $\;1\ \text{m}^3=1\,000\,000\ \text{cm}^3$ (cube it).
- Capacity: $1\ \text{ml}=1\ \text{cm}^3$, $\;1$ litre $=1\,000\ \text{cm}^3$.

<h4>4.7–4.8 Plans, elevations and problems</h4>

- **Plan** = view from above; **front elevation** = from the front; **side elevation** = from the side. Each is a 2D drawing with the true dimensions of that face.
- Packing problems: count how many fit along each dimension (whole numbers only) and multiply; empty space = container volume − boxes' volume.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A trapezium-shaped field has parallel sides 60 m and 90 m, 40 m apart. Find its area in hectares (1 ha $=10\,000$ m²).

$A=\tfrac{1}{2}(60+90)\times40=75\times40=3\,000$ m² $=0.3$ ha.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A prism is 12 cm long and its cross-section is a right-angled triangle with legs 5 cm and 8 cm. Find its volume and surface area (hypotenuse $\approx9.4$ cm).

Cross-section $\tfrac{1}{2}\times5\times8=20$ cm²; volume $20\times12=240$ cm³. Surface area: two triangles $2\times20=40$, three rectangles $12\times(5+8+9.4)=268.8$; total $308.8$ cm².
</div>

**Common slips:** using a sloping side as the height · forgetting the ½ in the triangle formula · $1\ \text{m}^2=100\ \text{cm}^2$ (it's $10\,000$) · counting the lid of an open box.
