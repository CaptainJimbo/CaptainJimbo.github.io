An **equation** has an unknown number (a letter) and an equals sign; *solving* it means finding that number. A **formula** is a rule written in symbols, like $P=2(l+w)$. The whole unit rests on one idea: **keep the two sides balanced**.

<h4>2.1 One-step equations</h4>

- A **function machine** applies a rule to an input; the **inverse** undoes it: the inverse of $+3$ is $-3$, of $\times5$ is $\div5$.
- **Balancing method:** do the same operation to both sides. $x+5=12 \to$ subtract 5 from both sides $\to x=7$. Check: $7+5=12$ ✓.
- Always **check** by putting your answer back into the original equation.

<h4>2.2 Two-step equations</h4>

- Undo the operations in **reverse order**. For $4x+7=27$: the machine does $\times4$ then $+7$, so undo $-7$ first ($4x=20$), then $\div4$ ($x=5$).
- **Brackets:** expand first — $2(x-1)=8 \to 2x-2=8 \to x=5$ — or divide both sides by the number outside when it divides exactly: $6(x+2)=30 \to x+2=5$.
- A fraction like $\dfrac{x+5}{2}=4$: multiply both sides by 2 first.
- "I think of a number…" → call it $n$ and write the calculation as an equation.

<h4>2.3 Letters on both sides</h4>

- Get all the letter terms on one side: subtract the **smaller** letter term from both sides. $3x-1=2x+5 \to$ subtract $2x$ $\to x-1=5 \to x=6$.
- The **coefficient** of $x$ is the number multiplying it: in $4x$ it is 4.
- Expand any brackets first, then balance.

<h4>2.4 Working with formulae</h4>

- **Substitute** the known values, then solve the equation that is left: $T=40m+20$ with $T=100 \to 100=40m+20 \to m=2$.
- **Write** a formula from words: "cost $C$ is £3 postage plus £$x$ per item" → $C=3+x$; "hours $h$ into days $d$" → $d=\dfrac{h}{24}$.
- An equation with **two unknowns** (e.g. $a+b=10$) has many solutions; list them systematically ($a=1,\ b=9$; $a=2,\ b=8$; …).

<h4>Angles you will need</h4>

- Angles on a **straight line** add up to $180^\circ$; angles in a **triangle** add up to $180^\circ$; angles around a **point** add up to $360^\circ$. Write the sum as an equation and solve.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Three angles on a straight line are $x$, $2x$ and $3x$. Find them.

$x+2x+3x=180 \Rightarrow 6x=180 \Rightarrow x=30$. The angles are $30^\circ$, $60^\circ$ and $90^\circ$.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Solve $\ 5(x-2)=3(x+4)$.

Expand: $5x-10=3x+12$. Subtract $3x$: $2x-10=12$. Add 10: $2x=22$, so $x=11$. Check: $5\times9=45$ and $3\times15=45$ ✓.
</div>

**Common slips:** undoing the operations in the wrong order · forgetting to multiply *every* term in a bracket · changing one side only · not checking the answer.
