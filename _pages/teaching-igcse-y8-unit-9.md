<h4>9.1 Quadrilaterals</h4>

| | sides | angles | diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Square** | all equal, 2 pairs parallel | all 90° | equal, bisect each other at 90° |
| **Rectangle** | opposite equal & parallel | all 90° | equal, bisect each other |
| **Rhombus** | all equal, 2 pairs parallel | opposite equal | bisect each other at 90° |
| **Parallelogram** | opposite equal & parallel | opposite equal | bisect each other |
| **Kite** | 2 pairs of equal *adjacent* sides | one pair of equal angles | cross at 90° |
| **Trapezium** | one pair parallel | — | — |

- Angles in any quadrilateral add to $360^\circ$. In a parallelogram, neighbouring angles add to $180^\circ$ (co-interior).

<h4>9.2 Angles and parallel lines</h4>

- Lines crossed by a transversal: **alternate** angles (Z shape) are **equal**; **corresponding** angles (F shape) are **equal**; **co-interior** angles (C shape) **add to $180^\circ$**.
- Plus the basics: angles on a straight line $180^\circ$, around a point $360^\circ$, vertically opposite equal, triangle $180^\circ$.
- Always write the reason next to the calculation.

<h4>9.3 Angles in polygons</h4>

- Interior angle sum of an $n$-sided polygon: $(n-2)\times180^\circ$ — triangle 180°, quadrilateral 360°, pentagon 540°, hexagon 720°. Works for reflex angles too.
- **Exterior angles** of any polygon add to $360^\circ$; interior + exterior at each vertex $=180^\circ$.
- **Regular** polygon: exterior $=\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$, interior $=180^\circ-\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$. Given an exterior angle, $n=360\div$ exterior.
- Joining the centre to the vertices makes $n$ isosceles triangles with angle $\dfrac{360^\circ}{n}$ at the centre.
- Regular polygons **tessellate** only if the interior angle divides $360^\circ$: triangles, squares, hexagons.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A regular polygon has interior angles of $150^\circ$. How many sides?

Exterior $=180-150=30^\circ$; $n=360\div30=12$ sides (a dodecagon).
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Three angles of a pentagon are $100^\circ$, $110^\circ$ and $120^\circ$; the other two are equal. Find them.

Sum $=540^\circ$; remaining $540-330=210^\circ$, so each is $105^\circ$.
</div>

**Common slips:** calling a rhombus a square · using "alternate" for angles that are actually co-interior · forgetting that exterior angles sum to 360° *whatever* the number of sides · dividing 360 by the interior angle instead of the exterior.
