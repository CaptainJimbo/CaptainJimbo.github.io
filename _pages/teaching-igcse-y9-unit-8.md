Maps, plans and models are all **scale drawings**: the real thing shrunk by a fixed scale factor. This unit covers reading scales, giving directions as **bearings**, and deciding when shapes are **congruent** or **similar**.

<h4>8.1 Maps and scales · 8.3 Scales and ratios</h4>

- A scale of **1 cm to 20 m** means every centimetre on the plan is 20 m in real life. Multiply to go from plan to real; divide to go from real to plan.
- A **ratio scale** such as $1:50\,000$ has no units: 1 cm represents $50\,000$ cm $=500$ m $=0.5$ km.
- Useful: $1$ m $=100$ cm and $1$ km $=100\,000$ cm.
- For an area, convert each **length** first, then multiply.

<h4>8.2 Bearings</h4>

- A **bearing** is an angle measured **from north**, **clockwise**, written with **three figures**: $065^\circ$, $225^\circ$.
- "The bearing of B **from** A" — stand at A, face north, turn clockwise until you face B.
- The **back bearing** differs by $180^\circ$: if B is on $065^\circ$ from A, then A is on $245^\circ$ from B.

<h4>8.4 Congruent and similar shapes</h4>

- **Congruent** shapes are identical in shape and size. Two triangles are congruent if you can show **SSS**, **SAS**, **ASA** or **RHS**.
- **Similar** shapes have the same angles and their sides are in the **same ratio** — one is an enlargement of the other. Equal angles alone (AAA) give similarity, not congruence.
- To find a missing side: scale factor $=\dfrac{\text{side in one shape}}{\text{matching side in the other}}$, then multiply.

<h4>8.5 Solving geometrical problems</h4>

- A line drawn parallel to one side of a triangle cuts off a **similar triangle** (the angles are corresponding angles).
- Use the **whole** side of the big triangle ($AB$, not $DB$) when you work out the scale factor.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> In triangle PQR, S lies on PQ and T lies on PR with ST parallel to QR. $PS=4$ cm, $SQ=6$ cm and $ST=5$ cm. Work out QR.

Triangles PST and PQR are similar (corresponding angles). $PQ=4+6=10$ cm, so the scale factor is $\dfrac{10}{4}=2.5$. $QR=2.5\times5=12.5$ cm.
</div>

**Common slips:** bearings written with two figures ($65^\circ$) or measured anticlockwise · using $DB$ instead of $AB$ for the scale factor in nested triangles · forgetting to convert centimetres to kilometres · claiming congruence from three equal angles · scaling an area by the length scale factor.
