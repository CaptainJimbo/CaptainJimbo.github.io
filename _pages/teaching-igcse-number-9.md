Money maths from everyday life: deciding which deal is **better value**, working with **sales tax** and **income-tax bands**, and converting between **currencies** at a given exchange rate.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>Comparative costs</h4>

- Compare **per unit**: cost per 100 g, per litre, km per litre of fuel. Divide to get the same unit for both options, then compare.
- Cars: km per litre $=\dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{fuel}}$ (bigger is better) or litres per 100 km (smaller is better).

<h4>Taxation</h4>

- **Sales tax** is a fixed percentage added to the price (often 15% or 20%). Price including tax $=$ original $\times(1+\text{rate})$; to recover the original, **divide** by $1.2$ (not subtract 20%).
- **Income tax** is charged in **bands**: a tax-free amount, then each slice of salary taxed at that band's rate. Work band by band and add. Convert yearly tax to weekly or monthly by dividing by 52 or 12.

<h4>Salaries</h4>

- A salary is quoted **per annum** (p.a.). Overtime, commission and bonuses are added before tax is worked out.

<h4>Foreign currency</h4>

- A rate such as £1 $=\text{€}1.17$ means multiply pounds by 1.17 to get euros, divide euros by 1.17 to get pounds.
- Between two foreign currencies, go **through** the common one: dollars → pounds → euros.
- Rates change: a "stronger" pound buys more foreign currency, so a fixed foreign price costs fewer pounds.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Tax-free allowance 10 000 dollars, then 20% up to 40 000, then 40%. Tax on a salary of 52 000 dollars?

$20\%\times30\,000=6000$ and $40\%\times12\,000=4800$: total 10 800 dollars a year, i.e. 900 dollars a month.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A jacket costs €96 including 20% tax. Find the price without tax, then convert it to pounds at £1 $=\text{€}1.20$.

$96\div1.2=\text{€}80$; $80\div1.20=£66.67$.
</div>

**Common slips:** comparing totals instead of unit prices · subtracting the tax percentage from the tax-inclusive price · taxing the whole salary at the top rate · multiplying when you should divide in a currency conversion.
