Three more circle facts to add to the theorems from Unit 2, each with a precise **reason** the examiner expects to see written down: the perpendicular from the centre to a chord, the **alternate segment theorem**, and the **intersecting chords** rule.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>Chords and the centre</h4>

- A **chord** is a straight line joining two points on the circle.
- The perpendicular from the centre to a chord **bisects** the chord; equally, the line from the centre to the mid-point of a chord is **perpendicular** to it. This creates a right-angled triangle (radius, half-chord, distance from centre) for Pythagoras.

<h4>Alternate segment theorem</h4>

- The angle between a **tangent** and a **chord** drawn from the point of contact is equal to the angle in the **alternate segment** (the angle at the circumference on the other side of the chord).
- Two angles at the point of contact (one each side of the chord) match the two angles of the inscribed triangle at the far vertices.

<h4>Intersecting chords</h4>

- Chords AB and CD crossing at P, **inside** the circle: $\text{AP}\times\text{PB}=\text{CP}\times\text{PD}$.
- The same rule when the lines meet at P **outside** the circle, with each length measured **from P** to the two points where the line cuts the circle: $\text{PA}\times\text{PB}=\text{PC}\times\text{PD}$.

<h4>Reasons you will quote (from Unit 2 as well)</h4>

| fact | reason to write |
|---|---|
| tangent ⟂ radius | "angle between tangent and radius is 90°" |
| $\angle$ at centre $=2\times\angle$ at circumference | "angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference" |
| angles in the same segment are equal | "angles in the same segment" |
| angle in a semicircle $=90°$ | "angle in a semicircle" |
| opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° | "cyclic quadrilateral" |
| two tangents from a point are equal | "tangents from an external point" |
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A tangent touches a circle at A. Chord AB makes 62° with the tangent and C is on the major arc. Find angle ACB.

Alternate segment theorem: $\angle\text{ACB}=62°$.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Chords AB and CD cross at P. AP $=9$, PB $=4$, CP $=6$. Find PD.

$9\times4=6\times\text{PD}\Rightarrow\text{PD}=6$.
</div>

**Common slips:** matching the tangent–chord angle with the wrong angle (it is the one *opposite* the chord, not adjacent) · with P outside, using the short segment instead of the full length from P · an answer with no reasons · assuming a line is a diameter when it only looks like one.
