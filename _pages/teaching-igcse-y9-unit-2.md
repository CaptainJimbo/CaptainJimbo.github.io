Prisms, circles and cylinders turn up everywhere from packaging to pipes. This unit brings together their **areas and volumes**, and adds one of the most useful results in geometry: **Pythagoras' theorem**.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>2.1–2.2 Prisms</h4>

- A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way through.
- **Volume of a prism** $=$ area of cross-section $\times$ length.
- **Surface area** $=$ the total area of all the faces — sketch the net and add: two ends plus the rectangles round the side.

<h4>2.3–2.4 Circles</h4>

- **Circumference** $C=\pi d=2\pi r$. **Area** $A=\pi r^{2}$ (square the *radius*, not the diameter).
- "In terms of $\pi$" means leave $\pi$ in the answer: a circle of radius 6 has area $36\pi$.
- For a semicircle or quarter circle, the perimeter includes the **straight edges** as well as the arc.

<h4>2.5 Cylinders</h4>

- A cylinder is a prism with a circular cross-section: $V=\pi r^{2}h$.
- **Total surface area** $=2\pi r^{2}+2\pi rh$ (two circles plus the curved surface, which unrolls into a rectangle $2\pi r$ by $h$).

<h4>2.6 Pythagoras' theorem</h4>

- In a right-angled triangle with hypotenuse $c$ (the longest side, opposite the right angle): $c^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}$.
- Finding the hypotenuse: **add** the squares. Finding a shorter side: **subtract** the squares.
- The distance between two points is the hypotenuse of a triangle whose shorter sides are the differences in the $x$- and $y$-coordinates.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A cylinder of radius 5 cm has volume $400\pi$ cm³. Work out its height and its total surface area in terms of $\pi$.

$V=\pi r^{2}h$ gives $400\pi=25\pi h$, so $h=16$ cm. Surface area $=2\pi\times5^{2}+2\pi\times5\times16=50\pi+160\pi=210\pi$ cm².
</div>

**Common slips:** using the diameter in $\pi r^{2}$ · forgetting the straight edge in the perimeter of a semicircle · adding the squares when the unknown is a *shorter* side · surface area of a prism with a face missing · cm² and cm³ mixed up.
