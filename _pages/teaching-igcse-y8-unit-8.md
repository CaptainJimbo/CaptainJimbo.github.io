Percentages, fractions and decimals are three ways of writing the same thing; ratios compare parts. Both come down to one skill — finding **one part** (or 1%) and scaling.

<div class="kp" markdown="1">
<h4>8.1–8.2 Equivalents and writing percentages</h4>

- Percentage → decimal: divide by 100 ($35\%=0.35$). Decimal → percentage: multiply by 100. Fraction → percentage: make the denominator 100, or divide and multiply by 100 ($\tfrac{7}{20}=\tfrac{35}{100}=35\%$).
- Percentages over 100% are fine: $1.25=125\%=1\tfrac{1}{4}$.
- To write one amount as a percentage of another: $\dfrac{\text{part}}{\text{whole}}\times100$. Use this to **compare groups of different sizes**.

<h4>8.3 Percentage of amounts</h4>

- Use a **multiplier**: $35\%$ of 480 $=0.35\times480$. Increase by 6% → $\times1.06$; decrease by 35% → $\times0.65$.
- **Reverse percentages** ("10% of an amount is 4.8 kg"): find 1% (or 10%), then 100%.
- **Simple interest:** the same interest every year — $4.5\%$ of €2400 $=€108$ per year.

<h4>8.4 Compound interest</h4>

- Interest is added each year and then **earns interest itself**: multiply by the multiplier once per year — €3000 at 2% for 3 years is $3000\times1.02^3$.
- Successive percentage changes **multiply**: 20% off then 30% off is $0.8\times0.7=0.56$ — a 44% discount, not 50%.

<h4>8.5–8.6 Ratios</h4>

- **Simplify** a ratio by dividing every part by the same number; clear decimals first ($2.5:7.5=1:3$).
- **Share in a ratio:** add the parts, divide the total by that to get one part, multiply. $3:7$ of 450 ml → 10 parts of 45 ml → 135 ml and 315 ml.
- Percentages ↔ ratios: 60% : 30% : 10% $=6:3:1$; fractions of the whole are $\tfrac{6}{10},\tfrac{3}{10},\tfrac{1}{10}$.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> A price is cut by 15% to €51. What was it?

€51 is 85% of the original. $1\%=51\div85=0.60$, so $100\%=€60$. Check: $60\times0.85=51$ ✓.
</div>

<div class="ex" markdown="1">
<b class="lbl">Worked example.</b> Share €720 between three people in the ratio $2:3:4$.

$2+3+4=9$ parts; $720\div9=80$ per part. Shares: €160, €240, €320 (total €720 ✓).
</div>

**Common slips:** adding percentages of different totals · using 0.35 as the multiplier for "35% off" (it's 0.65) · simple vs compound interest mixed up · sharing a ratio by giving each person the ratio number itself.
