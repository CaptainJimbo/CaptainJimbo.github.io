Shape and Space 6 — Chords · Alternate segment theorem · Intersecting chords

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. Diagrams are not drawn accurately. Give a reason for every angle fact you use.

  1. [3]O is the centre of a circle of radius 10 cm. AB is a chord of length 16 cm and M is the mid-point of AB.
    a Write down the length of OA.b Write down the length of AM.c Work out the length of OM.

    a OA is a radius: 10 cm.  b The line from the centre to the mid-point of a chord is perpendicular to it and bisects it: AM $=8$ cm.  c Pythagoras in the right-angled triangle OMA: $\text{OM}=\sqrt{10^2-8^2}=\sqrt{36}=6$ cm.

  2. [6]In each diagram find $x$. The lengths are in cm.
    a The chords AB and CD cross at P inside the circle. AP $=6$, PB $=4$, CP $=3$, PD $=x$.b P is outside the circle. PB $=4$, BA $=5$, PD $=3$, DC $=x$.

    a Intersecting chords: $\text{AP}\times\text{PB}=\text{CP}\times\text{PD}$, so $6\times4=3x\Rightarrow x=8$ cm.

    b The same rule with P outside — measure every length from P: $\text{PB}\times\text{PA}=\text{PD}\times\text{PC}$. $\text{PA}=4+5=9$ and $\text{PC}=3+x$: $4\times9=3(3+x)\Rightarrow 36=9+3x\Rightarrow x=9$ cm.

  3. [12]In each diagram find the angles $x$, $y$ and $z$. Give a reason for each step.
    a The line through A is a tangent. The angle between the tangent and the chord AB is 58°, and angle ABC $=47°$.b O is the centre and the line through A is a tangent. Angle OAB $=25°$.

    a $x=\angle\text{ACB}=58°$ — the angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment. $y=\angle\text{BAC}=180°-58°-47°=75°$ — angles in a triangle add up to 180°. $z=\angle\text{ABC}=47°$ — alternate segment theorem again, now for the chord AC (the angle between the tangent and AC equals the angle in the alternate segment).

    b Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA $=$ OB, radii), so $\angle\text{OBA}=25°$ and $x=\angle\text{AOB}=180°-50°=130°$. $y=90°-25°=65°$ — a tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact. $z=\angle\text{ACB}=65°$ — the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference ($130°\div2$), or equally by the alternate segment theorem, $z=y$.

  4. [4]TB and TC are tangents from T to the circle with centre O. A is a point on the major arc BC. Angle BTC $=40°$.
    a Find angle TBC.b Find angle BAC.c Find angle BOC.

    a Two tangents from the same point are equal, TB $=$ TC, so triangle TBC is isosceles: $\angle\text{TBC}=\frac{1}{2}(180°-40°)=70°$.

    b TB is a tangent and BC a chord, so by the alternate segment theorem $\angle\text{BAC}=\angle\text{TBC}=70°$.

    c Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference: $\angle\text{BOC}=2\times70°=140°$. (Check: in quadrilateral OBTC the angles at B and C are 90° each, so $\angle\text{BOC}=360°-90°-90°-40°=140°$ ✓.)

Total: 25 marks

Check: perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord (then Pythagoras) · alternate segment: tangent–chord angle = angle in the opposite segment · intersecting chords, inside or outside: $\text{AP}\times\text{PB}=\text{CP}\times\text{PD}$ with every length measured from the crossing point P · tangent ⟂ radius · two tangents from a point are equal.
