IGCSE Mathematics · Edexcel 4MA1 Higher · Unit 6

Sequences — $n$th terms · The difference method · Arithmetic sequences and series

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. $S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}\,[\,2a+(n-1)d\,]$.

  1. [3]Write down the first three terms of the sequence whose $n$th term is
    a $5n+2$b $n^2-n$c $\dfrac{2n+1}{n+2}$

    Substitute $n=1,2,3$. a $7,\ 12,\ 17$.  b $0,\ 2,\ 6$.  c $1,\ \tfrac{5}{4},\ \tfrac{7}{5}$.

  2. [3]Use the difference method to find the next three terms of each sequence.
    a $3,\ 7,\ 15,\ 27,\ 43,\ \dots$b $20,\ 17,\ 14,\ 11,\ \dots$c $1,\ 2,\ 4,\ 7,\ 11,\ \dots$

    a Differences $4,8,12,16$ — they go up by 4, so the next differences are $20,24,28$: $63,\ 87,\ 115$.  b Constant difference $-3$: $8,\ 5,\ 2$.  c Differences $1,2,3,4$, next $5,6,7$: $16,\ 22,\ 29$.

  3. [4]Find a formula for the $n$th term of each sequence.
    a $6,\ 10,\ 14,\ 18,\ \dots$b $93,\ 86,\ 79,\ 72,\ \dots$

    a The difference is constant, $4$, so the formula is $4n+b$. When $n=1$ it must equal 6: $4+b=6\Rightarrow b=2$. $n$th term $=4n+2$.

    b Constant difference $-7$, so $-7n+b$; $n=1$: $-7+b=93\Rightarrow b=100$. $n$th term $=100-7n$.

  4. [4]The 3rd term of an arithmetic sequence is 11 and the 8th term is 31. Find the first term $a$ and the common difference $d$.

    $n$th term $=a+(n-1)d$. So $a+2d=11$ and $a+7d=31$. Subtracting: $5d=20\Rightarrow d=4$. Then $a=11-8=3$.

  5. [5]The 6th term of an arithmetic sequence is 23 and the 15th term is 50. Find the sum of the first 30 terms.

    $a+5d=23$ and $a+14d=50$. Subtracting: $9d=27\Rightarrow d=3$, so $a=23-15=8$.

    $S_{30}=\dfrac{30}{2}\,[\,2\times8+29\times3\,]=15\times(16+87)=15\times103=1545$.

  6. [3]The sum of the first 24 terms of an arithmetic sequence is 1080. The common difference is 2. Find the first term.

    $S_{24}=\dfrac{24}{2}\,[\,2a+23\times2\,]=12\,(2a+46)=1080\Rightarrow 2a+46=90\Rightarrow a=22$.

  7. [3]Logs are stacked in rows. The bottom row has 30 logs, each row above has one log fewer than the row below, and the top row has 6 logs. How many logs are in the stack?

    The rows form an arithmetic sequence $30,29,\dots,6$: $a=30$, $d=-1$. Number of rows: $30-6+1=25$. Sum $=\dfrac{25}{2}\,(30+6)=25\times18=450$ logs. (Using the first-and-last form $S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}(a+l)$; the formula with $d$ gives the same: $\dfrac{25}{2}[60-24]=450$.)

Total: 25 marks

Check: a constant first difference $a$ means the $n$th term is $an+b$ — find $b$ from the first term · arithmetic sequence: $n$th term $a+(n-1)d$ · two given terms → two equations, subtract to find $d$ · $S_n=\dfrac{n}{2}[2a+(n-1)d]=\dfrac{n}{2}(a+l)$ · count the number of terms carefully (last − first + 1 when $d=\pm1$).
