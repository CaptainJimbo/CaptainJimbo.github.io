Graphs 5 — Inequalities on graphs · Perpendicular lines · Mid-points · Distances

Exercises

Squared paper needed. Shade the regions not wanted, as in the exam. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question.

  1. [2]Illustrate the inequality $-2

    Draw $x=-2$ as a dotted line (not included) and $x=4$ as a solid line (included). Shade everything to the left of $x=-2$ and to the right of $x=4$; the strip between them is the region.

  2. [8]
    • a Draw axes from 0 to 10 and on them draw the lines $x=5$, $y=7$ and $2x+3y=24$.
    • b Shade the regions not satisfied by $x\le5$, $y\le7$, $2x+3y\le24$, $x\ge0$, $y\ge0$.
    • c Write down the point with integer coordinates in the unshaded region that is furthest from the origin.

    a $x=5$ vertical, $y=7$ horizontal; $2x+3y=24$ through $(0,8)$ and $(12,0)$ — use $(0,8)$, $(3,6)$, $(9,2)$. All solid lines.

    b Keep the region below $y=7$, left of $x=5$, below the sloping line, in the first quadrant.

    c Candidates near the far corner: $(1,7)$: $2+21=23\le24$ ✓, distance $\sqrt{50}=7.07$; $(3,6)$: on the line, distance $\sqrt{45}=6.71$; $(5,4)$: $\sqrt{41}=6.40$. Furthest: $(1,7)$. ($(2,7)$ gives $25>24$ — outside.)

  3. [7]$A$ is $(2,\,-3)$ and $B$ is $(6,\,5)$.
    • a Find the length of $AB$.
    • b Find the mid-point of $AB$.
    • c Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of $AB$.

    a $\sqrt{(6-2)^2+(5-(-3))^2}=\sqrt{16+64}=\sqrt{80}=8.94$ (3 s.f.).

    b $\left(\dfrac{2+6}{2},\dfrac{-3+5}{2}\right)=(4,\,1)$.

    c Gradient of $AB$ $=\dfrac{8}{4}=2$, so the perpendicular gradient is $-\tfrac{1}{2}$. Through $(4,1)$: $1=-\tfrac{1}{2}(4)+c \Rightarrow c=3$. Equation: $y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+3$.

  4. [8]$A$ is $(1,\,-4)$ and $B$ is $(7,\,4)$. $AB$ is a diameter of a circle with centre $C$.
    • a Find the coordinates of $C$.
    • b Find the radius of the circle.
    • c Show that the point $D(8,\,3)$ lies on the circle.
    • d A circle theorem says that $AD$ is perpendicular to $BD$. Show that this is true.

    a Mid-point of $AB$: $(4,\,0)$.

    b $AB=\sqrt{36+64}=10$, so the radius is 5.

    c $CD=\sqrt{(8-4)^2+(3-0)^2}=\sqrt{25}=5$ = the radius, so $D$ is on the circle.

    d Gradient $AD=\dfrac{3-(-4)}{8-1}=1$; gradient $BD=\dfrac{3-4}{8-7}=-1$. Product $=-1$, so the lines are perpendicular — the angle in a semicircle is $90^\circ$.

  5. [3]Find the equation of the line perpendicular to $y=3x-2$ that passes through $(6,\,1)$.

    Perpendicular gradient $-\tfrac{1}{3}$. $1=-\tfrac{1}{3}(6)+c \Rightarrow c=3$. $y=-\tfrac{1}{3}x+3$.

Total: 28 marks

Check: solid line for $\le$ / $\ge$, dotted for $<$ / $>$ · test a point to decide which side · perpendicular gradients multiply to $-1$ · mid-point = mean of the coordinates · distance = Pythagoras on the coordinate differences.
