Number 7 — Recurring decimals · Advanced calculator problems

Exercises

Question 1 and 4 without a calculator. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. Dot notation: $0.\dot{2}\dot{7}=0.272727\dots$

  1. [12]Convert each recurring decimal to a fraction in its simplest form.
    a $0.\dot{7}$b $0.\dot{2}\dot{7}$c $0.\dot{4}2\dot{3}$d $0.0\dot{4}\dot{5}$e $0.8\dot{3}$f $2.1\dot{6}$

    Let $x$ be the decimal; multiply by 10, 100 or 1000 (one, two or three repeating digits) and subtract so the repeating part cancels.

    a $10x-x=7.\dot7-0.\dot7=7\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{7}{9}$

    b $100x-x=27\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{27}{99}=\dfrac{3}{11}$

    c $1000x-x=423\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{423}{999}=\dfrac{47}{111}$

    d $100x=4.\dot4\dot5$ and $x=0.0\dot4\dot5$: $99x=4.5\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{4.5}{99}=\dfrac{45}{990}=\dfrac{1}{22}$

    e $10x=8.\dot3$, $100x=83.\dot3$: $90x=75\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{75}{90}=\dfrac{5}{6}$

    f $10x=21.\dot6$, $100x=216.\dot6$: $90x=195\Rightarrow x=\dfrac{195}{90}=\dfrac{13}{6}=2\tfrac{1}{6}$

  2. [6]Calculate, giving each answer correct to 3 significant figures.
    a $\dfrac{1}{2^3}+\dfrac{1}{3^3}+\dfrac{1}{4^3}$b $\dfrac{6.4\times10^4}{8.15\times10^6}$c $\sqrt[5]{\dfrac{7.2\times10^4}{3.1\times10^{-2}}}$

    a $0.125+0.037037+0.015625=0.178$.  b $0.007853\ldots=7.85\times10^{-3}$.  c Inside the root: $2.32\times10^6$; fifth root (use the $\sqrt[x]{\ }$ key or power $\tfrac{1}{5}$): $18.8$.

  3. [4]Find the value of $z$ to 3 significant figures, given that $z=\sqrt[3]{\dfrac{a^2+b^2+c^2}{\tan(b-c)^\circ}}$ where $a=40$, $b=50$ and $c=20$.

    Numerator $1600+2500+400=4500$; denominator $\tan30^\circ=0.5774$; quotient $7794.2$; cube root: $z=19.8$. Keep the full calculator value until the final rounding.

  4. [3]$a$ and $b$ are digits. Show that $1.\dot{a}\dot{b}=\dfrac{ab+99}{99}$, where $ab$ is the two-digit number with digits $a$ and $b$.

    Let $N=1.abab\ldots$ Two digits repeat, so multiply by 100: $100N=1ab.abab\ldots$ Subtracting, $99N=1ab-1$. The three-digit number $1ab$ is $100+ab$, so $99N=100+ab-1=ab+99$ and $N=\dfrac{ab+99}{99}$. (Check with $a=2$, $b=7$: $\dfrac{126}{99}=1.2727\ldots$ ✓)

Total: 25 marks

Check: one repeating digit → ×10, two → ×100, three → ×1000, then subtract · a non-repeating digit after the point: multiply by 10 first, then by 10 again (×100) and subtract · always simplify the fraction · calculator: brackets round numerators and denominators, keep full accuracy, round at the end.
