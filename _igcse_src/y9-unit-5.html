Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 9 · Unit 5

Inequalities, equations and formulae — Substitution · Inequalities · Index laws · Equations · Changing the subject

Exercises

No calculator needed. ★ = challenge question.

  1. Work out the value of each expression.
    • a $3a+b^{2}$ when $a=4$, $b=5$
    • b $50-(a+b^{3})$ when $a=6$, $b=3$
    • c $(a+b)^{2}-3c$ when $a=2$, $b=6$, $c=7$
    • d $\dfrac{3a^{2}}{bc}$ when $a=4$, $b=2$, $c=6$
    • e $x^{2}(5-y)-2z$ when $x=3$, $y=-1$, $z=4$

    a $12+25=37$   b $50-(6+27)=17$   c $8^{2}-21=43$

    d $\dfrac{3\times16}{12}=4$   e $9\times(5+1)-8=54-8=46$

  2. $x$ is an integer.
    • a List the values of $x$ for which $-2\lt x\le3$.
    • b List the values of $x$ for which $-4\le x\lt0$.
    • c Describe how to show $x\le-1$ on a number line.
    • d Describe how to show $-2\lt x\le4$ on a number line.

    a $-1,\ 0,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3$   b $-4,\ -3,\ -2,\ -1$

    c A filled (closed) circle at $-1$ with an arrow pointing left.   d An empty (open) circle at $-2$, a filled circle at $4$, and a line joining them.

  3. Solve these inequalities.
    a$x+5\lt12$b$4y\ge-20$c$\dfrac{x}{3}-1\gt2$d$-7\le2x+1\lt9$e$-x\gt-8$f$5-2x\le11$

    a $x\lt7$   b $y\ge-5$   c $\dfrac{x}{3}\gt3$, so $x\gt9$   d Subtract 1: $-8\le2x\lt8$; divide by 2: $-4\le x\lt4$

    e Multiply by $-1$ and reverse the sign: $x\lt8$   f $-2x\le6$; divide by $-2$ and reverse: $x\ge-3$

  4. Simplify.
    a$x^{5}\times x^{3}$b$y^{9}\div y^{4}$c$(z^{3})^{4}$d$6a^{0}$e$\dfrac{q^{4}}{q^{10}}$f$3p^{2}\times4p^{-5}$g$(2m^{3})^{3}$

    a $x^{8}$   b $y^{5}$   c $z^{12}$   d $6\times1=6$

    e $q^{4-10}=q^{-6}$   f $12p^{2-5}=12p^{-3}$   g $2^{3}m^{9}=8m^{9}$

  5. Say whether each of these is an expression, an equation, an identity or a formula.
    a$3x+7$b$3x+7=19$c$3(x+2)\equiv3x+6$d$A=\pi r^{2}$

    a expression (no equals sign)   b equation (true for one value, $x=4$)   c identity (true for every value of $x$)   d formula (a rule connecting two or more variables)

  6. Solve.
    a$5x-7=3x+9$b$4(2x-1)=3(x+7)$c$\dfrac{3x+5}{4}=5$d$2x+1=\dfrac{7x-9}{3}$e$\dfrac{5x+1}{4}=\dfrac{4x+5}{3}$

    a $2x=16$, $x=8$   b $8x-4=3x+21$, $5x=25$, $x=5$   c $3x+5=20$, $x=5$

    d Multiply by 3: $6x+3=7x-9$, so $x=12$   e Cross-multiply: $3(5x+1)=4(4x+5)$, $15x+3=16x+20$, $x=-17$

  7. Factorise completely.
    a$18x+12$b$15x^{4}-10x^{2}$c$y^{2}+12xy+8yz$d$6x^{2}-9xy-3xz$

    a $6(3x+2)$   b $5x^{2}(3x^{2}-2)$   c $y(y+12x+8z)$   d $3x(2x-3y-z)$

  8. Use the formula $v=u+at$.
    • a Work out $v$ when $u=5$, $a=3$ and $t=4$.
    • b Work out $t$ when $v=30$, $u=6$ and $a=4$.
    • c Make $a$ the subject of the formula.

    a $v=5+3\times4=17$

    b $30=6+4t$, $4t=24$, $t=6$

    c $v-u=at$, so $a=\dfrac{v-u}{t}$

  9. Make $x$ the subject of each formula.
    a$y=x-c$b$P=kx$c$h=4x+7$d$A=mx^{2}$e$9x=xy+q$

    a $x=y+c$   b $x=\dfrac{P}{k}$   c $x=\dfrac{h-7}{4}$   d $x^{2}=\dfrac{A}{m}$, so $x=\sqrt{\dfrac{A}{m}}$

    e Collect the $x$ terms: $9x-xy=q$, $x(9-y)=q$, $x=\dfrac{q}{9-y}$

  10. An isosceles triangle has two sides of length $(2x+3)$ cm and a base of length $(x+4)$ cm. Its perimeter is at least 25 cm but less than 40 cm.
    • a Write an expression for the perimeter. Simplify it.
    • b Write an inequality for the perimeter and solve it.
    • c $x$ is an integer. Write down its possible values.

    a $(2x+3)+(2x+3)+(x+4)=5x+10$

    b $25\le5x+10\lt40$. Subtract 10: $15\le5x\lt30$. Divide by 5: $3\le x\lt6$.

    c $x=3,\ 4$ or $5$.

  11. A taxi company charges a fixed fee of €4 plus €1.50 per kilometre.
    • a Write a formula for the cost, $C$ euros, of a journey of $d$ km.
    • b Work out the cost of a 12 km journey.
    • c Make $d$ the subject of the formula.
    • d Sam has €25. Write and solve an inequality to find how far he can travel.

    a $C=4+1.5d$

    b $C=4+1.5\times12=22$, so €22.

    c $C-4=1.5d$, so $d=\dfrac{C-4}{1.5}$

    d $4+1.5d\le25$, $1.5d\le21$, $d\le14$. He can travel at most 14 km.

Check: multiplying or dividing an inequality by a negative number reverses the sign · $a^{0}=1$ · to change the subject, undo the operations in reverse order · if the new subject appears twice, collect its terms and factorise.
