Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 9 · Unit 6

Collecting and analysing data — Surveys · Sampling · Two-way tables · Averages from grouped data · Scatter graphs

Exercises

Questions marked calc may be done with a calculator. ★ = challenge question.

  1. A school wants to find out what students think of its new canteen. Two of the survey questions are:
    A  “Do you agree that the new, improved canteen is much better?”
    B  “How much do you spend on lunch?”   ☐ €0–€2   ☐ €2–€4   ☐ €4–€6
    • a Explain what is wrong with each question.
    • b Write a better version of each question.

    a A is a leading question — “new, improved” and “do you agree” push people towards yes. B has overlapping classes (€2 and €4 are each in two boxes), no box for more than €6, and no time period (per day? per week?).

    b For example: “How would you rate the canteen?” ☐ poor ☐ OK ☐ good ☐ excellent. “How much do you spend on lunch on a typical school day?” ☐ less than €2 ☐ €2 to €3.99 ☐ €4 to €5.99 ☐ €6 or more.

  2. A school has 1200 students. Maya wants to survey a 10% sample about homework.
    • a How many students should she ask?
    • b Maya plans to ask her friends in Year 9. Explain why this is not a good sample.
    • c Describe a better way to choose the sample.
    • d Is her data primary or secondary?

    a $10\%$ of $1200=120$ students.

    b It is biased: her friends are all the same age and likely to have similar views, so they do not represent the whole school.

    c For example: number every student from 1 to 1200 and use a random number generator to pick 120, so every student has an equal chance of being chosen.

    d Primary — she collects it herself.

  3. Leo measures the reaction times of his classmates. Most are between 0.2 and 0.5 seconds.
    • a How accurately should he record the times?   A nearest second   B nearest 0.1 s   C nearest 0.01 s. Give a reason.
    • b Design a grouped frequency table for his data.

    a C — to the nearest 0.01 s. The other two would round almost every result to the same value.

    b For example, equal classes with no gaps or overlaps: $0.1\le t\lt0.2$, $0.2\le t\lt0.3$, $0.3\le t\lt0.4$, $0.4\le t\lt0.5$, $0.5\le t\lt0.6$, with a tally column and a frequency column.

  4. The two-way table shows how 60 students travel to school.
    WalkBusCarTotal
    Boys12928
    Girls148
    Total2260
    • a Copy and complete the table.
    • b What fraction of the students walk? Give your answer in its simplest form.
    • c What percentage of the girls travel by bus?

    WalkBusCarTotal
    Boys129728
    Girls1014832
    Total22231560

    a Boys by car $28-12-9=7$; girls who walk $22-12=10$; girls total $60-28=32$; bus total $23$; car total $15$.

    b $\dfrac{22}{60}=\dfrac{11}{30}$   c $\dfrac{14}{32}\times100=43.75\%$

  5. calc The table shows the times taken by 40 students to travel to school.
    Time, $t$ (minutes)Frequency
    $0\lt t\le10$5
    $10\lt t\le20$13
    $20\lt t\le30$14
    $30\lt t\le40$8
    • a Write down the modal class.
    • b Calculate an estimate of the mean.
    • c Which class contains the median?
    • d Estimate the range.
    • e Describe how to draw a frequency polygon for this data.

    ClassMidpointFrequencyMidpoint × frequency
    $0\lt t\le10$5525
    $10\lt t\le20$1513195
    $20\lt t\le30$2514350
    $30\lt t\le40$358280
    Total40850

    a $20\lt t\le30$ (highest frequency).   b Estimated mean $=\dfrac{850}{40}=21.25$ minutes.

    c The median is between the 20th and 21st values. The first two classes hold $5+13=18$ values, so both are in $20\lt t\le30$.

    d Largest possible $-$ smallest possible $=40-0=40$ minutes.

    e Plot frequency against the midpoint of each class — $(5,5)$, $(15,13)$, $(25,14)$, $(35,8)$ — and join the points with straight lines.

  6. The amounts, in euros, that six friends spent at a fair were   $12,\ 15,\ 14,\ 16,\ 13,\ 48$.
    • a Work out the mean and the median.
    • b Which average represents the data better? Explain.

    a Mean $=\dfrac{118}{6}=19.67$ (2 d.p.). In order: $12,13,14,15,16,48$; median $=\dfrac{14+15}{2}=14.5$.

    b The median. The outlier 48 pulls the mean above five of the six values, so the mean is not typical.

  7. Class A has a mean test score of 62 with a range of 45. Class B has a mean of 58 with a range of 20. Write two sentences comparing the classes.

    On average class A scored higher (mean 62 against 58). Class B's scores were more consistent, because its range is much smaller (20 against 45).

  8. The table shows the hours of revision and the test scores of seven students.
    Hours of revision1234567
    Test score35425055647040
    • a Describe the correlation.
    • b Write down the coordinates of the outlier.
    • c A line of best fit is drawn, ignoring the outlier. Estimate the score of a student who revises for 4.5 hours.
    • d Explain why the line should not be used to predict the score of a student who revises for 15 hours.

    a Positive correlation — more revision generally goes with a higher score.

    b $(7,40)$: a lot of revision but a low score.

    c Ignoring the outlier, the scores rise by about 7 marks per hour; a line of best fit gives about 60 marks.

    d 15 hours is far outside the range of the data (extrapolation). The pattern may not continue, and the line would predict a score of over 100.

  9. The mean of five numbers is 12. A sixth number is added and the mean becomes 13. Work out the sixth number.

    Total of five numbers $=5\times12=60$. Total of six numbers $=6\times13=78$. Sixth number $=78-60=18$.

  10. calc Two companies each record the delivery times of 50 parcels.
    Time, $t$ (minutes)Company ACompany B
    $0\le t\lt20$410
    $20\le t\lt40$1825
    $40\le t\lt60$2210
    $60\le t\lt80$65
    • a Write down the modal class for each company.
    • b Calculate an estimate of the mean delivery time for each company.
    • c Write two sentences comparing the companies.

    a A: $40\le t\lt60$. B: $20\le t\lt40$.

    b Midpoints $10,30,50,70$. A: $\dfrac{40+540+1100+420}{50}=\dfrac{2100}{50}=42$ minutes. B: $\dfrac{100+750+500+350}{50}=\dfrac{1700}{50}=34$ minutes.

    c Company B is faster on average (estimated mean 34 minutes against 42, and a lower modal class). Both have the same estimated range (80 minutes), so their spread is similar.

Check: classes must not overlap or leave gaps · estimated mean = total of (midpoint × frequency) ÷ total frequency · compare with one average and one measure of spread · never predict far outside the range of the data.
