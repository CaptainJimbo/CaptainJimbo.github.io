Graphs 6 — Cubic graphs · Reciprocal graphs

Exercises

Calculator allowed; graph paper needed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. Plot the points and join them with a smooth curve — never with a ruler.

  1. [6]a Copy and complete the table of values for $y=x^3-3x^2-x+3$, then draw the graph for $-2\le x\le4$.  b Write down the values of $x$ where the graph cuts the $x$-axis.
    $x$-2-101234
    $y$-153-315

    a Substitute carefully, cubing negatives: $(-1)^3=-1$.

    $x$-2-101234
    $y$-15030-3015

    A positive cubic: down on the left, up on the right, with a "hill" and a "valley".  b The graph cuts the $x$-axis at $x=-1$, $x=1$ and $x=3$ (where $y=0$).

  2. [9]The depth of water $D$ metres in a tidal pool, $t$ hours after 6 a.m., is given by $D=t^3-6t^2+9t+2$ for $0\le t\le4$.
    a Copy and complete the table and draw the graph of $D$ against $t$.b Use your graph to estimate the greatest depth and the time when it occurs.c Between which times is the depth less than 3 m?
    $t$ (hours)01234
    $D$ (m)246

    a

    $t$ (hours)01234
    $D$ (m)26426

    b The curve peaks at $t=1$: greatest depth $6$ m, at 7 a.m. (It dips to 2 m at $t=3$, 9 a.m., then rises again.)

    c Draw the line $D=3$ and read where the curve is below it: from about $t=2.4$ to $t=3.5$, i.e. roughly 8:20 a.m. to 9:30 a.m. (Any sensible reading from an accurate graph is accepted — e.g. $2.3\le t\le3.6$.)

  3. [10]The cost £$y$ per item of producing $x$ hundred items is modelled by $y=\dfrac{x}{2}+\dfrac{8}{x}$ for $1\le x\le8$.
    a Copy and complete the table (2 d.p.).b Draw the graph for $1\le x\le8$, with $y$ from 0 to 9.

    b The curve falls steeply, flattens near $x=4$, then rises slowly — the $\dfrac{8}{x}$ term dominates for small $x$, the $\dfrac{x}{2}$ term for large $x$.

    d The minimum is at $x=4$, i.e. 400 items, costing $\dfrac{4}{2}+\dfrac{8}{4}=£4$ per item.

Total: 25 marks

Check: build a table row by row ($x^3$, $-3x^2$, …) and add · cubes of negatives are negative · smooth curve through every point, no straight segments · a reciprocal graph never touches $x=0$ · read a graph by drawing a horizontal or vertical line and marking the crossing.
