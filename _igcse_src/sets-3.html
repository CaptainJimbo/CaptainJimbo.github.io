IGCSE Mathematics · Edexcel 4MA1 Higher · Unit 7

Sets 3 — Probability from Venn diagrams · Conditional probability

Exercises

Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. $P(A)=\dfrac{n(A)}{n(\mathscr{E})}$; $P(A\mid B)$ = probability of $A$ given $B$. Give probabilities as fractions in simplest form.

  1. [5]In a class of 30 students, 21 passed a Physics test (P) and 24 passed a Chemistry test (C). 2 students failed both.
    a Draw a Venn diagram.b A student is chosen at random. Find i $P(\text{P})$  ii $P(\text{P}\cap\text{C})$  iii $P(\text{P}\cup\text{C})$  iv $P(\text{P}'\cap\text{C})$.

    a Both $=x$: $(21-x)+x+(24-x)+2=30\Rightarrow x=17$. Regions: P only 4, both 17, C only 7, neither 2.

    b i $\dfrac{21}{30}=\dfrac{7}{10}$  ii $\dfrac{17}{30}$  iii $\dfrac{4+17+7}{30}=\dfrac{28}{30}=\dfrac{14}{15}$  iv C but not P: $\dfrac{7}{30}$.

  2. [5]A café records what 150 customers buy one morning: 120 buy a coffee, 70 buy a cake, and every customer buys at least one of the two. A customer is picked at random. Find the probability that they bought
    a a coffee and a cakeb a coffee or a cake but not both.

    Both $=120+70-150=40$ (nobody is outside). Coffee only $80$, cake only $30$. a $\dfrac{40}{150}=\dfrac{4}{15}$.  b $\dfrac{80+30}{150}=\dfrac{110}{150}=\dfrac{11}{15}$.

  3. [5]In a survey, 80% of students own a bike (B), 30% own a skateboard (S) and 15% own neither. A student is chosen at random. Find the probability that the student
    a owns a bike or a skateboard but not bothb owns a skateboard, given that they own a bike.

    Work in percentages: both $=80+30-(100-15)=25\%$. Bike only $55\%$, skateboard only $5\%$, neither $15\%$. a $\dfrac{55+5}{100}=\dfrac{3}{5}$.  b Restrict to bike owners (80%): $P(\text{S}\mid\text{B})=\dfrac{25}{80}=\dfrac{5}{16}$.

  4. [5]The Venn diagram shows which of football (F), hockey (H) and tennis (T) the students in a year group play.
    a How many students are in the year group?b A student is chosen at random. Find i $P(\text{H})$  ii $P(\text{F}\cap\text{H}\cap\text{T})$  iii $P(\text{T}\mid\text{H})$.

    a $18+6+10+7+4+3+5+7=60$.  b i $n(\text{H})=6+10+4+3=23$: $\dfrac{23}{60}$.  ii $\dfrac{4}{60}=\dfrac{1}{15}$.  iii Given H, only the 23 hockey players count; of them $4+3=7$ play tennis: $\dfrac{7}{23}$.

  5. [5]A music club has 40 members, each of whom plays at least one of piano (P), guitar (G) and drums (D). 24 play piano, 20 play guitar and 15 play drums; 9 play piano and guitar, 7 play guitar and drums, 8 play piano and drums. A member is chosen at random. Find
    a $P(\text{P}\cap\text{G}\cap\text{D})$b $P(\text{D}\mid\text{G})$.

    Let $x$ play all three. Pairs only: $9-x$, $7-x$, $8-x$. Singles: P only $24-(9-x)-(8-x)-x=7+x$, G only $20-(9-x)-(7-x)-x=4+x$, D only $15-(7-x)-(8-x)-x=x$. Total: $(7+x)+(4+x)+x+(9-x)+(7-x)+(8-x)+x=35+x=40\Rightarrow x=5$.

    a $\dfrac{5}{40}=\dfrac{1}{8}$.  b Among the 20 guitarists, those who also play drums are $(7-x)+x=7$: $P(\text{D}\mid\text{G})=\dfrac{7}{20}$.

Total: 25 marks

Check: $P(A\cup B)$ counts the overlap once · "but not both" = the two outer parts · $P(A\mid B)=\dfrac{{n(A\cap B)}}{{n(B)}}$ — the denominator is the given set, not the whole universal set · percentages work like counts out of 100 · simplify every fraction.
