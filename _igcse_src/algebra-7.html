Algebra 7 — Solving quadratic equations · Quadratic inequalities

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. Quadratic formula: $x=\dfrac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}$.

  1. [5]Solve by factorising.
    a $3x^2-48=0$b $2x^2+5x-3=0$c $x(3x-7)=6$

    a $3(x^2-16)=0\Rightarrow x^2=16\Rightarrow x=\pm4$.  b $(2x-1)(x+3)=0\Rightarrow x=\tfrac12$ or $x=-3$.  c Expand and bring everything to one side first: $3x^2-7x-6=0\Rightarrow(3x+2)(x-3)=0\Rightarrow x=-\tfrac23$ or $x=3$.

  2. [4]a Complete the square for $x^2-6x+4$.  b Hence solve $x^2-6x+4=0$, giving your answers in surd form.

    a $x^2-6x+4=(x-3)^2-9+4=(x-3)^2-5$.  b $(x-3)^2=5\Rightarrow x-3=\pm\sqrt5\Rightarrow x=3+\sqrt5$ or $x=3-\sqrt5$.

  3. [4]Use the quadratic formula to solve, giving answers in surd form.
    a $x^2+3x-2=0$b $3x^2-5x+1=0$

    a $a=1,b=3,c=-2$: $x=\dfrac{-3\pm\sqrt{9+8}}{2}=\dfrac{-3\pm\sqrt{17}}{2}$.  b $a=3,b=-5,c=1$: $x=\dfrac{5\pm\sqrt{25-12}}{6}=\dfrac{5\pm\sqrt{13}}{6}$.

  4. [4]Solve each quadratic inequality and show the solution set on a number line.
    a $3x^2>27$b $x^2-2x-8\le0$

    Sketch the parabola and read off where it is above or below the $x$-axis.

    a $x^2>9$: the graph of $y=x^2-9$ is above the axis outside the roots $\pm3$, so $x<-3$ or $x>3$ — two separate parts (open circles at $-3$ and $3$, arrows outwards).  b $(x-4)(x+2)\le0$: roots $-2$ and $4$; the U-shaped graph is below (or on) the axis between them, so $-2\le x\le4$ — one part (filled circles, line between).

  5. [4]Two chords of a circle cross as shown. a Use the intersecting chords theorem to form an equation in $x$. b Solve it to find $x$, correct to 3 significant figures.

    a $x(x+2)=3\times3\Rightarrow x^2+2x-9=0$.  b $x=\dfrac{-2\pm\sqrt{4+36}}{2}=-1\pm\sqrt{10}$. A length is positive, so $x=-1+\sqrt{10}=2.16$ (3 s.f.).

  6. [4]A rectangular poster is 4 cm longer than it is wide. Its area is 60 cm². Find its width.

    Width $w$, length $w+4$: $w(w+4)=60\Rightarrow w^2+4w-60=0\Rightarrow(w+10)(w-6)=0$. $w=-10$ is impossible, so the width is $6$ cm (and the length 10 cm; check $6\times10=60$ ✓).

Total: 25 marks

Check: bring everything to one side (= 0) before factorising · completing the square: $(x+\tfrac b2)^2-(\tfrac b2)^2+c$ · formula: write $a,b,c$ with signs, $b^2$ is positive, $-4ac$ positive if $a$ or $c$ is negative · inequality: sketch, roots, "below" = between, "above" = outside · reject negative lengths.
