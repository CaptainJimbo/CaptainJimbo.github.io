Graphs 9 — Gradient of a function · Differentiation · Stationary points · Motion in a straight line

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. If $y=kx^n$ then $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=nkx^{n-1}$.

  1. [7]Find the gradient of the tangent to each curve at $x=1$.
    a $y=2x^3+5x-1$b $y=(x-1)(4x+3)$c $y=3-\dfrac{4}{x^2}$

    a $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=6x^2+5$; at $x=1$: $11$.  b Expand first: $y=4x^2-x-3$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=8x-1$; at $x=1$: $7$.  c $y=3-4x^{-2}$, $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=8x^{-3}=\dfrac{8}{x^3}$; at $x=1$: $8$.

  2. [6]Find the stationary points of the curve $y=x^3-6x^2+9x+1$ and say whether each is a maximum or a minimum.

    $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=3x^2-12x+9=3(x^2-4x+3)=3(x-1)(x-3)=0\Rightarrow x=1$ or $x=3$.

    $x=1$: $y=1-6+9+1=5$. $x=3$: $y=27-54+27+1=1$. Stationary points $(1,5)$ and $(3,1)$.

    Classify by the gradient either side: at $x=0$ the gradient is $9>0$, at $x=2$ it is $-3<0$, at $x=4$ it is $9>0$. So $(1,5)$ is a maximum (gradient goes $+\,0\,-$) and $(3,1)$ a minimum ($-\,0\,+$) — the usual shape of a positive cubic.

  3. [6]The fuel consumption $y$ km per litre of a car travelling at $x$ km/h is modelled by $y=60-\dfrac{x}{4}-\dfrac{1600}{x}$ for $40\le x\le120$. a Find an expression for $\dfrac{dy}{dx}$. b Find the most economical speed and the fuel consumption at that speed.

    a $y=60-\tfrac14x-1600x^{-1}$, so $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=-\dfrac14+1600x^{-2}=-\dfrac14+\dfrac{1600}{x^2}$.

    b Maximum where $\dfrac{dy}{dx}=0$: $\dfrac{1600}{x^2}=\dfrac14\Rightarrow x^2=6400\Rightarrow x=80$ km/h (the positive root in range). Consumption $y=60-20-20=20$ km per litre. (It is a maximum: for $x<80$ the gradient is positive, for $x>80$ negative.)

  4. [6]The displacement $s$ metres of a particle from a fixed point O after $t$ seconds is $s=6\sqrt t+2t+5$ for $0\le t\le4$. Find, when $t=1$, the particle's a velocity in m/s  b acceleration in m/s².

    a Velocity $=\dfrac{ds}{dt}$. Write $s=6t^{\frac12}+2t+5$: $v=3t^{-\frac12}+2=\dfrac{3}{\sqrt t}+2$. At $t=1$: $v=5$ m/s.

    b Acceleration $=\dfrac{dv}{dt}=-\dfrac32t^{-\frac32}=-\dfrac{3}{2\sqrt{t^3}}$. At $t=1$: $a=-1.5$ m/s² (slowing down).

Total: 25 marks

Check: multiply by the power, then reduce the power by 1; constants vanish · expand brackets or rewrite $\frac{a}{x^n}$ as $ax^{-n}$ and $\sqrt x$ as $x^{\frac12}$ before differentiating · stationary point: $\frac{dy}{dx}=0$; classify by gradient either side · velocity $=\frac{ds}{dt}$, acceleration $=\frac{dv}{dt}$.
