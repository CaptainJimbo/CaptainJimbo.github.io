Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 8 · Unit 11

Straight-line graphs — Plotting · Gradient · $y=mx+c$

Exercises

Squared paper needed. ★ = challenge question.

  1. Draw axes from $-6$ to $6$. Draw the graph of $y=2x-3$ using a table of values for $x=-1,0,1,2,3$.

    $x=-1,0,1,2,3 \to y=-5,-3,-1,1,3$. Plot and join with a ruler: a straight line crossing the $y$-axis at $-3$, going up 2 for every 1 across.

  2. Draw the graph of $3x+2y=6$.

    Easiest: $x=0 \Rightarrow y=3$; $y=0 \Rightarrow x=2$. Join $(0,3)$ and $(2,0)$. A third check point: $x=4 \Rightarrow 2y=-6 \Rightarrow y=-3$.

  3. Four lines: $y=x$, $\;y=3x$, $\;y=2x+4$, $\;y=2x-1$.
    • a Which lines pass through the origin?
    • b Which line is the steepest?
    • c Which two lines are parallel?
    • d Which line crosses the $y$-axis lowest down?

    a $y=x$ and $y=3x$ (no "$+c$", so $c=0$).  b $y=3x$ — largest gradient.  c $y=2x+4$ and $y=2x-1$ — same gradient 2.  d $y=2x-1$, at $(0,-1)$.

  4. Work out the gradient of the line through each pair of points.
    a $(0,1)$ and $(2,5)$b $(-2,3)$ and $(4,0)$c $(1,-4)$ and $(3,-4)$

    Gradient $=\dfrac{\text{change in }y}{\text{change in }x}$. a $\dfrac{4}{2}=2$  b $\dfrac{-3}{6}=-\dfrac{1}{2}$  c $\dfrac{0}{2}=0$ — a horizontal line.

  5. A line passes through $(0,-2)$ and $(4,6)$. Write down its equation.

    Gradient $=\dfrac{6-(-2)}{4-0}=2$; it crosses the $y$-axis at $-2$. Equation: $y=2x-2$.

    • a Where does the line $y=3x-5$ cross the $y$-axis?
    • b What is the gradient of the line $y=-2x+7$?
    • c Write the equation of the line with gradient 4 that crosses the $y$-axis at $(0,-3)$.

    a At $(0,-5)$.  b $-2$ (falling).  c $y=4x-3$.

  7. Does the point $(6,2)$ lie on the line $y=2x-14$? Show working to explain.

    Put $x=6$ into the equation: $y=12-14=-2$, not 2. So $(6,2)$ is not on the line; $(6,-2)$ is.

  8. Write the equation of each line.
    • a Through $(0,3)$ and $(3,0)$.
    • b Through $(0,2)$ and $(1,5)$.
    • c Through $(0,-3)$ and $(4,-1)$.

    a Gradient $\dfrac{0-3}{3-0}=-1$, intercept 3: $y=-x+3$.  b Gradient 3, intercept 2: $y=3x+2$.  c Gradient $\dfrac{2}{4}=\tfrac{1}{2}$, intercept $-3$: $y=\tfrac{1}{2}x-3$.

  9. A line $y=mx+c$ passes through the point $(1,3)$. Write down three possible equations for the line. What is the relationship between $m$ and $c$?

    Substituting $x=1$, $y=3$: $3=m+c$. Any pair with $m+c=3$ works: $y=x+2$, $y=2x+1$, $y=-x+4$, … — all these lines pass through $(1,3)$.

  10. Line A is $y=2x+1$. Line B is parallel to A and passes through $(3,2)$. Line C is $y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+1$.
    • a Find the equation of line B.
    • b Where do lines A and C cross?

    a Same gradient 2: $y=2x+c$; $2=6+c \Rightarrow c=-4$. $y=2x-4$.

    b Both have intercept 1, so they cross on the $y$-axis at $(0,1)$. (Algebra: $2x+1=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+1 \Rightarrow x=0$.)

Check: $y=mx+c$ — $m$ is the gradient, $c$ where it crosses the $y$-axis · parallel lines have the same $m$ · a point is on the line only if its coordinates satisfy the equation.
