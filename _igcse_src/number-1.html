Number 1 — Fractions · Order of operations · Rounding

Exercises

No calculator. Give answers as fractions or mixed numbers in their lowest terms unless the question says otherwise. Marks are shown in brackets. ★ = harder question (grades 7–9).

A · Fractions

  1. [3]Simplify
    a $\dfrac{18}{48}$b $\dfrac{150}{48}$c $\dfrac{2.4}{48}$

    a Divide top and bottom by the HCF, 6: $\dfrac{18}{48}=\dfrac{3}{8}$.

    b $\dfrac{150}{48}=\dfrac{25}{8}=3\tfrac{1}{8}$ (HCF 6).

    c Multiply top and bottom by 10 first: $\dfrac{2.4}{48}=\dfrac{24}{480}=\dfrac{1}{20}$.

  2. [4]Work out
    a $\dfrac{4}{9}\times1\tfrac{1}{8}$b $3\tfrac{1}{3}\div\dfrac{5}{6}$

    a Convert to an improper fraction, then cancel before multiplying: $\dfrac{4}{9}\times\dfrac{9}{8}=\dfrac{4\times9}{9\times8}=\dfrac{4}{8}=\dfrac{1}{2}$.

    b To divide, multiply by the reciprocal: $\dfrac{10}{3}\div\dfrac{5}{6}=\dfrac{10}{3}\times\dfrac{6}{5}=\dfrac{60}{15}=4$.

  3. [3]Work out $\dfrac{5}{6}+2\tfrac{3}{4}-1\tfrac{2}{3}$

    Improper fractions, then a common denominator (LCM of 6, 4, 3 is 12): $\dfrac{5}{6}+\dfrac{11}{4}-\dfrac{5}{3}=\dfrac{10}{12}+\dfrac{33}{12}-\dfrac{20}{12}=\dfrac{23}{12}=1\tfrac{11}{12}$.

  4. [5]In a school, $\dfrac{2}{5}$ of the students study Spanish, $\dfrac{1}{4}$ study French, $\dfrac{1}{10}$ study German and the rest study no language.
    • a What fraction of the students study no language?
    • b 90 students study no language. How many students are in the school?

    a Common denominator 20: $\dfrac{8}{20}+\dfrac{5}{20}+\dfrac{2}{20}=\dfrac{15}{20}$, so the rest is $1-\dfrac{15}{20}=\dfrac{5}{20}=\dfrac{1}{4}$.

    b $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of the school is 90 students, so the school has $90\times4=360$ students.

  5. [3]A jug holds $3\tfrac{3}{4}$ litres of juice. A cup holds $\dfrac{3}{8}$ litre. How many full cups can be poured from the jug?

    "How many $\tfrac{3}{8}$ in $3\tfrac{3}{4}$" is a division: $\dfrac{15}{4}\div\dfrac{3}{8}=\dfrac{15}{4}\times\dfrac{8}{3}=\dfrac{120}{12}=10$ cups.

B · Order of operations

  1. [4]Work out
    a $5+3\times(2+3)^2$b $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{1}{3}\div\dfrac{4}{9}$

    a Brackets, then indices, then multiply, then add: $5+3\times5^2=5+3\times25=5+75=80$.

    b Division before addition: $\dfrac{1}{3}\div\dfrac{4}{9}=\dfrac{1}{3}\times\dfrac{9}{4}=\dfrac{3}{4}$, so $\dfrac{2}{3}+\dfrac{3}{4}=\dfrac{8}{12}+\dfrac{9}{12}=\dfrac{17}{12}=1\tfrac{5}{12}$.

  2. [2]Insert one pair of brackets to make this calculation correct: $\;2+3\times4-1=19$

    Without brackets the value is $2+12-1=13$. Try forcing the addition first: $(2+3)\times4-1=5\times4-1=19$ ✓.

  3. [3]Evaluate $\left(1\tfrac{1}{2}-\dfrac{2}{3}\right)^2\div\left(\dfrac{5}{6}+\dfrac{1}{4}\right)$

    First bracket: $\dfrac{9}{6}-\dfrac{4}{6}=\dfrac{5}{6}$, squared: $\dfrac{25}{36}$. Second bracket: $\dfrac{10}{12}+\dfrac{3}{12}=\dfrac{13}{12}$.

    $\dfrac{25}{36}\div\dfrac{13}{12}=\dfrac{25}{36}\times\dfrac{12}{13}=\dfrac{25}{39}$.

C · Significant figures and decimal places

  1. [2]The height of Mount Everest is $8\,848.86$ m. Write this height correct to
    a 1 decimal placeb 2 significant figures

    a The 2nd d.p. is 6 (≥ 5), so round up: $8\,848.9$ m.

    b The first two s.f. are 8 and 8; the 3rd s.f. is 4 (< 5), so leave them: $8\,800$ m. (The zeros keep the size of the number.)

  2. [2]$\pi=3.14159265\ldots$  Write $\pi$ correct to
    a 4 significant figuresb 4 decimal places

    a 4 s.f.: look at the 5th s.f., 5 → round up: $3.142$.

    b 4 d.p.: look at the 5th d.p., 9 → round up: $3.1416$.

  3. [3]The mass of a grain of pollen is $0.000\,004\,09$ g.
    • a Write this mass correct to 1 significant figure.
    • b Write it correct to 6 decimal places.
    • c Explain why your answer to b is less useful than your answer to a.

    a The first s.f. is the 4; the next digit is 0, so $0.000\,004$ g.

    b 6 d.p.: $0.000\,004\,09 \to 0.000\,004$ (the 7th d.p. is 0). Same digits, but…

    c …to 6 d.p. the answer has only one non-zero digit left, so it says nothing about the 9; s.f. follow the size of the number, d.p. do not.

D · Mixed problems

  1. [5]A bag of rice weighs $2\tfrac{2}{5}$ kg.
    • a A café uses $\dfrac{3}{10}$ kg of rice each day. For how many full days does the bag last?
    • b A different café uses $\dfrac{1}{3}$ of a new bag in the first week and then $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of what is left in the second week. What mass of rice is left after two weeks?

    a $\dfrac{12}{5}\div\dfrac{3}{10}=\dfrac{12}{5}\times\dfrac{10}{3}=\dfrac{120}{15}=8$ days.

    b After week 1 the fraction left is $1-\dfrac{1}{3}=\dfrac{2}{3}$. Week 2 uses $\dfrac{3}{4}$ of that, so $\dfrac{1}{4}$ of it remains: $\dfrac{2}{3}\times\dfrac{1}{4}=\dfrac{1}{6}$ of the bag. Mass: $\dfrac{1}{6}\times\dfrac{12}{5}=\dfrac{2}{5}$ kg.

  2. [3]Kai says: "$-3^2$ and $(-3)^2$ are the same because both contain $-3$ and a square." Is Kai right? Work out both values and explain.

    Indices come before the minus sign (which here means $-1\times$): $-3^2=-(3\times3)=-9$. With brackets the square applies to $-3$: $(-3)^2=(-3)\times(-3)=9$. Kai is wrong — the bracket changes which operation is done first.
