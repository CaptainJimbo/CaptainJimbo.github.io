Shape and Space 7 — Circles and sectors · Solids · Similar shapes

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question. Give answers to 3 s.f. unless exact. Area of a circle $\pi r^2$, circumference $2\pi r$.

  1. [4]A running track is a rectangle 20 m by 10 m with a semicircle on each 10 m end. Find its perimeter and its area.

    The two semicircles make one full circle of diameter 10 m (radius 5 m). Perimeter $=2\times20+\pi\times10=40+31.4=71.4$ m. Area $=20\times10+\pi\times5^2=200+78.5=279$ m².

  2. [4]A sector of a circle of radius 9 cm has angle 140°. Find its area and its perimeter.

    Area $=\dfrac{140}{360}\times\pi\times9^2=99.0$ cm². Arc length $=\dfrac{140}{360}\times2\pi\times9=21.99$ cm, so perimeter $=21.99+9+9=40.0$ cm (two radii as well as the arc).

  3. [5]A hollow concrete pipe is 5 m long. Its outer diameter is 80 cm and its inner diameter is 60 cm. Find the volume of concrete in m³.

    Work in metres: radii 0.4 m and 0.3 m. Cross-section (a ring) $=\pi(0.4^2-0.3^2)=\pi\times0.07=0.2199$ m². Volume $=$ cross-section $\times$ length $=0.2199\times5=1.10$ m³.

  4. [6]Two bottles are similar. The smaller is 12 cm tall and the larger 18 cm tall.
    a The surface area of the smaller bottle is 150 cm². Find the surface area of the larger.b The volume of the larger bottle is 810 cm³. Find the volume of the smaller.

    Linear scale factor $k=\dfrac{18}{12}=1.5$. a Areas scale by $k^2=2.25$: $150\times2.25=337.5$ cm².  b Volumes scale by $k^3=3.375$: $810\div3.375=240$ cm³.

  5. [6]Cone A is 20 cm tall with volume 600 cm³ and curved surface area 320 cm². Cone B is similar to A and has volume 1500 cm³.
    a Find the height of cone B.b Find the curved surface area of cone B.

    Volume scale factor $k^3=\dfrac{1500}{600}=2.5$, so $k=\sqrt[3]{2.5}=1.3572$. a Height $=20\times1.3572=27.1$ cm.  b Area $=320\times k^2=320\times1.8420=589$ cm².

Total: 25 marks

Check: sector: $\frac{{\theta}}{{360}}$ of the area or of the circumference; perimeter adds two radii · prism/cylinder: cross-section × length · hollow shapes: subtract areas, then multiply by length · similar shapes: lengths ×$k$, areas ×$k^2$, volumes ×$k^3$; from a volume ratio take the cube root to get $k$.
