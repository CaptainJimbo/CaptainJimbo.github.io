Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 9 · Unit 4

Constructions — Constructing triangles · Bisectors · Perpendiculars · Nets

Exercises

You need a sharp pencil, a ruler, a protractor and a pair of compasses. Leave all construction arcs on your diagrams. ★ = challenge question.

  1. Use a ruler and compasses to construct a triangle with sides 7 cm, 6 cm and 4 cm. Measure its largest angle.

    Draw the 7 cm side. Open the compasses to 6 cm and draw an arc from one end; open them to 4 cm and draw an arc from the other end. Join the ends to the point where the arcs cross. The largest angle is opposite the 7 cm side: about $86^\circ$.

  2. Use a ruler and protractor to draw triangle ABC with $AB=8$ cm, angle $A=50^\circ$ and angle $B=60^\circ$. Measure AC.

    Draw AB, measure $50^\circ$ at A and $60^\circ$ at B, and extend the two lines until they meet at C. Angle $C=70^\circ$ and $AC\approx7.4$ cm.

  3. Draw triangle PQR with $PQ=6$ cm, $PR=5$ cm and angle $P=40^\circ$. Measure QR.

    Draw PQ, measure $40^\circ$ at P, mark R 5 cm along that line and join R to Q. $QR\approx3.9$ cm.

  4. Draw a line 9 cm long. Construct its perpendicular bisector. Leave your construction arcs.

    Open the compasses to more than half of 9 cm. Draw arcs above and below the line from each end, keeping the same radius. Join the two points where the arcs cross. Check: the bisector crosses the line at 4.5 cm and at $90^\circ$.

  5. Draw an angle of $70^\circ$ with a protractor. Construct its bisector with compasses and check your answer.

    Draw an arc centred on the vertex that crosses both arms. From each crossing point draw an arc (same radius) so the two arcs meet. Join the vertex to that point. Each half should measure $35^\circ$.

  6. Use only a ruler and compasses.
    • a Construct an angle of $60^\circ$.
    • b Use it to construct an angle of $30^\circ$.
    • c Construct an angle of $90^\circ$, then an angle of $45^\circ$.

    a Construct an equilateral triangle (all three sides the same compass setting) — each angle is $60^\circ$.

    b Bisect the $60^\circ$ angle.

    c Construct the perpendicular bisector of a line ($90^\circ$), then bisect the right angle to get $45^\circ$.

  7. Construct triangle ABC with $AB=10$ cm, $BC=8$ cm and $AC=6$ cm.
    • a Measure angle C. What do you notice?
    • b Construct the perpendicular from C to AB.
    • c Measure the length of the perpendicular. What does this length represent?

    a $90^\circ$, because $6^{2}+8^{2}=10^{2}$.

    b With centre C, draw an arc that cuts AB twice; from those two points draw arcs below AB that cross; join C to the crossing point.

    c About $4.8$ cm. (Check with area: $\tfrac12\times6\times8=24=\tfrac12\times10\times h$, so $h=4.8$.) The perpendicular distance is the shortest distance from a point to a line.

  8. Explain why it is impossible to construct a triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 9 cm.

    The two shorter sides add to $3+4=7$ cm, which is less than 9 cm, so the arcs from the ends of the 9 cm side never meet. The two shorter sides of a triangle must add to more than the longest side.

  9. A triangular prism has a right-angled triangle with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm as its cross-section. The prism is 6 cm long.
    • a Construct an accurate net of the prism.
    • b Use your net to work out the surface area of the prism.

    a Three rectangles side by side (3 by 6, 4 by 6 and 5 by 6) with a 3-4-5 triangle on each end of the 5 by 6 (or any) rectangle; construct the triangles with compasses.

    b Two triangles: $2\times\tfrac12\times3\times4=12$. Rectangles: $(3+4+5)\times6=72$. Surface area $=84$ cm².

  10. A rectangular garden ABCD has $AB=12$ m and $AD=8$ m. A sprinkler is placed so that it is the same distance from A as from B, and the same distance from side AB as from side AD.
    • a Make a scale drawing of the garden, using 1 cm to represent 2 m.
    • b Construct the position of the sprinkler. Say which two constructions you used.
    • c How far is the sprinkler from A in real life?

    a Draw a rectangle 6 cm by 4 cm.

    b Same distance from A and B: on the perpendicular bisector of AB. Same distance from AB and AD: on the bisector of angle A. The sprinkler is where the two constructions cross.

    c It is 3 cm along and 3 cm up on the drawing, about 4.2 cm from A, so $\approx8.5$ m in real life. (Exactly $\sqrt{6^{2}+6^{2}}=8.49$ m.)

  11. Construct a triangle with sides 6 cm, 8 cm and 10 cm. Construct the perpendicular bisector of each side.
    • a What do you notice about the three bisectors?
    • b Draw the circle that passes through all three vertices. What is its radius?

    a All three bisectors meet at one point — the midpoint of the 10 cm side.

    b A circle centred there passes through all three corners; its radius is $5$ cm, half the longest side.

Check: never rub out construction arcs · keep the compass radius fixed while you draw a matching pair of arcs · perpendicular bisector = same distance from two points · angle bisector = same distance from two lines.
