Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 8 · Unit 7

Probability — Experimental probability · Estimating probability

Exercises

Give probabilities as fractions, decimals or percentages. ★ = challenge question.

  1. Mia dropped a plastic cup 40 times and recorded how it landed.
    OutcomeFrequencyExperimental probability
    on its side29
    upright8
    upside down3
    • a Copy and complete the table.
    • b Mia predicted that the cup would be unlikely to land upright. Was she right? Explain.
    • c How could Mia make her estimates more reliable?

    a Experimental probability $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{\text{total}}$: side $\dfrac{29}{40}=0.725$, upright $\dfrac{8}{40}=0.2$, upside down $\dfrac{3}{40}=0.075$.

    b Yes — upright happened in only 20% of drops, which is unlikely (less than $\tfrac{1}{2}$, and much less than landing on its side).

    c Repeat the experiment many more times — the more trials, the closer the estimate.

  2. In an honesty experiment, 80 wallets were left in a street. 32 were handed in at a shop, 28 at a police station and 20 were kept.
    • a Work out the experimental probability of each outcome.
    • b Another wallet is left in the street. Estimate the probability that it is handed in somewhere.

    a Shop $\dfrac{32}{80}=0.4$, police $\dfrac{28}{80}=0.35$, kept $\dfrac{20}{80}=0.25$ (they add to 1 ✓).

    b Handed in $=0.4+0.35=0.75$.

  3. In a video game a bonus item appears at random 3 times in every 24 levels. Work out the probability that the bonus appears in a particular level.

    $\dfrac{3}{24}=\dfrac{1}{8}=0.125$.

  4. In a survey, 8% of people who set themselves a fitness target achieved it. Another survey questions 350 people with a fitness target. How many would you expect to achieve it?

    Expected number $=$ probability $\times$ number of trials $=0.08\times350=28$ people.

  5. A bakery bakes 500 loaves. 15 are burnt and thrown away, 380 are sold at full price and the rest are sold at a discount.
    • a Work out the relative frequency of each outcome.
    • b Estimate the probability that a loaf is not burnt.
    • c Next week the bakery bakes 2000 loaves. How many would you expect to be sold at a discount?

    a Discounted $=500-15-380=105$. Burnt $\dfrac{15}{500}=0.03$, full price $\dfrac{380}{500}=0.76$, discounted $\dfrac{105}{500}=0.21$.

    b $1-0.03=0.97$.

    c $0.21\times2000=420$ loaves.

  6. The grades of 300 students in an exam were: A* 60, A 90, B 75, C 45, D 20, E 10.
    • a How many students got grade B or C?
    • b Estimate the probability that a student next year gets grade B or C.
    • c 150 students sit the exam next year. How many would you expect to get grade B or C?

    a $75+45=120$.  b $\dfrac{120}{300}=0.4$.  c $0.4\times150=60$ students.

  7. A dice is rolled 60 times and lands on 6 fifteen times.
    • a How many sixes would you expect from a fair dice in 60 rolls?
    • b What is the experimental probability of a 6?
    • c Does this prove the dice is biased? Explain.

    a Theoretical probability $\tfrac{1}{6}$: $\tfrac{1}{6}\times60=10$.

    b $\dfrac{15}{60}=0.25$ — higher than $\tfrac{1}{6}\approx0.17$.

    c No. 60 rolls is a small number of trials, and a fair dice can easily give 15 sixes by chance. Roll it many more times (say 600); if the proportion stays near 0.25, that would be evidence of bias.

  8. A spinner has three sectors of different sizes: red, blue and green. In 200 spins the results were red 96, blue 64, green 40.
    • a Estimate the probability of each colour.
    • b Which sector is the largest? Explain.
    • c How many greens would you expect in 1000 spins?

    a Red $0.48$, blue $0.32$, green $0.20$.

    b Red — it came up most often, so it is the most likely outcome, which means the biggest sector (about half the spinner).

    c $0.2\times1000=200$.

Check: experimental probability = frequency ÷ total trials · all the probabilities of an experiment add up to 1 · expected number = probability × trials · more trials → better estimate.
