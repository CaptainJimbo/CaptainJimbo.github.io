Graphs 3 — Distance–time graphs · Speed–time graphs

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Squared paper for the drawing question. State units in every answer. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question.

  1. [6]The travel graph shows Anjali's journey to a friend's house and back.

    She leaves at 0 min, arrives (6 km) at 4 min, stays until 9 min, and is home at 12 min.

    • a Calculate her outward speed in km/min.
    • b How long did she stay at her friend's house?
    • c Calculate her return speed in km/h.

    a Gradient of the first section: $\dfrac{6}{4}=1.5$ km/min.

    b The flat part: $9-4=5$ minutes.

    c Return: 6 km in 3 min $=2$ km/min $=2\times60=120$ km/h (the graph slopes down because she is coming back).

  2. [10]A drone accelerates steadily from rest to 15 m/s in 25 s, flies at that speed for 35 s, then slows steadily to rest in a further 40 s.
    • a Draw the speed–time graph of the flight.
    • b Use the graph to find the drone's (i) initial acceleration, (ii) final acceleration, (iii) average speed over the whole 100 s flight.

    a A trapezium: $(0,0)$ up to $(25,15)$, level to $(60,15)$, down to $(100,0)$.

    b (i) Gradient $=\dfrac{15}{25}=0.6$ m/s².  (ii) $\dfrac{0-15}{40}=-0.375$ m/s² (a deceleration of 0.375 m/s²).

    (iii) Distance = area under the graph: $\tfrac{1}{2}\times25\times15+35\times15+\tfrac{1}{2}\times40\times15=187.5+525+300=1012.5$ m. Average speed $=\dfrac{1012.5}{100}=10.1$ m/s (3 s.f.).

  3. [9]The winner of a 400 m race finished in 50.0 seconds.
    • a Calculate his average speed in m/s.
    • b Change your answer to km/h.
    • c The sketch of his speed–time graph shows him accelerating uniformly from rest for the first 6 seconds, then running at a constant maximum speed for the rest of the race. Calculate his maximum speed, correct to 3 s.f.
    • d Calculate his acceleration over the first 6 seconds, with units, correct to 2 s.f.

    a $\dfrac{400}{50}=8$ m/s.

    b $8\times3600\div1000=28.8$ km/h.

    c Let the maximum speed be $v$. Distance = area: triangle $\tfrac{1}{2}\times6\times v$ plus rectangle $44\times v$, so $3v+44v=47v=400 \Rightarrow v=8.51$ m/s.

    d $\dfrac{8.51}{6}=1.4$ m/s² (2 s.f.).

  4. [5]A train leaves a station and accelerates uniformly for 2 minutes until it reaches 30 m/s, travels at 30 m/s for 10 minutes, then decelerates uniformly to rest in 90 seconds at the next station. Calculate the distance between the stations in kilometres.

    Work in seconds: $120$ s, $600$ s, $90$ s. Area under the speed–time graph: $\tfrac{1}{2}\times120\times30+600\times30+\tfrac{1}{2}\times90\times30=1800+18\,000+1350=21\,150$ m $=21.15$ km.

Total: 30 marks

Check: distance–time — gradient = speed, flat = stopped, down = returning · speed–time — gradient = acceleration, area = distance · convert minutes to seconds (or hours) before calculating · m/s → km/h: × 3.6.
