Shape and Space 2 — Pythagoras' theorem · Circle theorems

Exercises

Calculator allowed. Give lengths to 3 s.f. unless exact. For every angle, give the circle theorem or angle fact you used. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question.

A · Pythagoras' theorem

  1. [4]In each right-angled triangle, find the missing side.
    • a The two shorter sides are 9 cm and 12 cm. Find the hypotenuse.
    • b The hypotenuse is 13 cm and one shorter side is 5 cm. Find the other side.

    a $c^2=9^2+12^2=81+144=225$, so $c=15$ cm.

    b $b^2=13^2-5^2=169-25=144$, so $b=12$ cm (subtract when finding a shorter side).

  2. [3]A ramp 3 m long leads up to a step 30 cm high. How far from the step does the ramp start? Give your answer in metres to the nearest centimetre.

    Same units: 30 cm $=0.3$ m. The ramp is the hypotenuse: $x^2=3^2-0.3^2=9-0.09=8.91$, so $x=\sqrt{8.91}=2.985\ldots\approx2.98$ m.

  3. [2]Is a triangle with sides 7 cm, 24 cm and 25 cm right-angled? Show how you decide.

    $7^2+24^2=49+576=625=25^2$. The longest side squared equals the sum of the other two squared, so by the converse of Pythagoras it is right-angled (the right angle is opposite the 25 cm side).

  4. [3]Find the distance between the points $A(1,\,2)$ and $B(7,\,10)$.

    Horizontal change $7-1=6$, vertical change $10-2=8$ — the legs of a right-angled triangle. $AB=\sqrt{6^2+8^2}=\sqrt{100}=10$.

  5. [2]A 6.5 m ladder leans against a wall and reaches 6 m up it. How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall?

    $d^2=6.5^2-6^2=42.25-36=6.25$, so $d=2.5$ m.

B · Circle theorems

  1. [9]In each diagram $O$ is the centre of the circle. Work out the angles marked with letters, giving a reason for each.

    Left: $AB$ is a diameter, angle $CAB=35^\circ$; find $a$ (angle $ABC$). Second: angle $QPR=48^\circ$; find $b$ (angle $QOR$). Third: chords $KN$ and $LM$; angle $KLM=62^\circ$; find $c$ (angle $KNM$). Right: cyclic quadrilateral $WXYZ$ with $W=106^\circ$, $X=78^\circ$; find $d$ (angle $Y$) and $e$ (angle $Z$).

    Left: angle $ACB=90^\circ$ (angle in a semicircle), so $a=180-90-35=55^\circ$ (angle sum of a triangle).

    Second: $b=2\times48=96^\circ$ (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc $QR$).

    Third: $c=62^\circ$ (angles in the same segment are equal — both stand on chord $KM$).

    Right: $d=180-106=74^\circ$ and $e=180-78=102^\circ$ (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to $180^\circ$).

  2. [3]$A$ and $B$ lie on a circle with centre $O$, and angle $AOB=130^\circ$. $C$ is a point on the major arc.
    • a Work out angle $ACB$.
    • b Work out angle $OAB$.

    a Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference: $ACB=65^\circ$.

    b $OA=OB$ (radii), so triangle $OAB$ is isosceles: $OAB=\dfrac{180-130}{2}=25^\circ$.

  3. [3]Find the area of an equilateral triangle with perimeter 30 cm.

    Side $=10$ cm. The height splits it into two right-angled triangles with hypotenuse 10 and base 5: $h=\sqrt{10^2-5^2}=\sqrt{75}=8.66$ cm. Area $=\tfrac{1}{2}\times10\times8.66=43.3$ cm² (3 s.f.).
