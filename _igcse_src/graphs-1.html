Graphs 1 — Gradient · Straight-line graphs · Conversion graphs

Exercises

You will need squared paper (or a printed grid) for the questions that say draw. Marks are shown in brackets. ★ = harder question.

A · Gradient

  1. [4]Find the gradient of the line joining each pair of points.
    • a $A(1,\,2)$ and $B(4,\,11)$
    • b $C(-3,\,4)$ and $D(5,\,-2)$

    Gradient $=\dfrac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}=\dfrac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}$.

    a $\dfrac{11-2}{4-1}=\dfrac{9}{3}=3$.  b $\dfrac{-2-4}{5-(-3)}=\dfrac{-6}{8}=-\dfrac{3}{4}$ — negative, the line slopes down from left to right.

  2. [3]A wheelchair ramp rises 1.2 m over a horizontal distance of 8 m.
    • a Work out the gradient of the ramp.
    • b Building rules say a ramp should have a gradient of at most $\dfrac{1}{12}$. How long (horizontally) must a ramp with the same rise be to meet the rule?

    a Gradient $=\dfrac{1.2}{8}=0.15$ (or $\dfrac{3}{20}$).

    b $\dfrac{1.2}{\text{run}}=\dfrac{1}{12} \Rightarrow \text{run}=1.2\times12=14.4$ m.

B · Straight-line graphs

  1. [5]For the line $y=3x-2$:
    • a Write down its gradient and its $y$-intercept.
    • b Copy and complete the table of values for $x=-1,\,0,\,1,\,2,\,3$.
    • c Draw the graph for $-1\le x\le3$.
    • d Use your graph (or the equation) to find where the line crosses the $x$-axis.

    a Compare with $y=mx+c$: gradient $3$, $y$-intercept $-2$, i.e. the point $(0,-2)$.

    b $x=-1,0,1,2,3 \to y=-5,-2,1,4,7$.

    c Plot the five points; they lie on a straight line rising 3 for every 1 across.

    d On the $x$-axis $y=0$: $3x-2=0 \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{2}{3}\approx0.67$. The crossing point is $\left(\tfrac{2}{3},0\right)$.

  2. [4]A line has equation $2x+5y=10$.
    • a Find the points where it crosses the two axes.
    • b Draw the line.
    • c Work out its gradient.

    a $x=0 \Rightarrow 5y=10 \Rightarrow y=2$, point $(0,2)$. $y=0 \Rightarrow 2x=10 \Rightarrow x=5$, point $(5,0)$.

    b Join $(0,2)$ to $(5,0)$ and extend.

    c From $(0,2)$ to $(5,0)$: $\dfrac{0-2}{5-0}=-\dfrac{2}{5}$. (Rearranging: $y=-\tfrac{2}{5}x+2$ gives the same.)

  3. [3]Here are four lines:  $y=2x+1$,  $2y=x+6$,  $y-2x=5$,  $x+2y=4$.
    Which two lines are parallel? Explain how you know.

    Write each as $y=mx+c$: $y=2x+1$ ($m=2$); $y=\tfrac{1}{2}x+3$ ($m=\tfrac12$); $y=2x+5$ ($m=2$); $y=-\tfrac{1}{2}x+2$ ($m=-\tfrac12$). Parallel lines have equal gradients, so $y=2x+1$ and $y-2x=5$ are parallel.

C · Real-life and conversion graphs

  1. [6]A taxi charges a fixed €3 plus €1.50 per kilometre, so the cost $C$ (euros) of a journey of $d$ km is $C=3+1.5d$.
    • a Draw the graph of $C$ against $d$ for $0\le d\le20$.
    • b What is the gradient of the graph, and what does it represent?
    • c Use your graph to find how far you can travel for €21.

    a Three points are enough for a straight line: $(0,3)$, $(10,18)$, $(20,33)$. Put $d$ on the horizontal axis and $C$ on the vertical axis.

    b Gradient $1.5$ — the cost per kilometre (€/km). The $C$-intercept 3 is the fixed charge.

    c Read across from $C=21$: $d=12$ km. Check: $3+1.5\times12=21$ ✓

  2. [5]5 miles is approximately 8 kilometres.
    • a Draw a conversion graph from miles to kilometres for $0$ to $50$ miles.
    • b Use it to convert 35 miles to kilometres.
    • c Use it to convert 100 km to miles.

    a A straight line through the origin and $(50,\,80)$ (since $50$ miles $=80$ km). Miles on the horizontal axis.

    b $35$ miles $\to 56$ km (exactly $35\times\tfrac{8}{5}$).

    c $100$ km $\to 62.5$ miles ($100\times\tfrac{5}{8}$). Readings from a graph are approximate — $62$ or $63$ miles is fine.

  3. [5]A candle is 24 cm tall and burns down at a steady 2 cm per hour.
    • a Write an equation for the height $h$ cm of the candle $t$ hours after it is lit.
    • b Draw the graph of $h$ against $t$ for $0\le t\le12$.
    • c Use your graph to find when the candle is a quarter of its original height.

    a $h=24-2t$.

    b Straight line from $(0,24)$ down to $(12,0)$ — gradient $-2$.

    c A quarter of 24 cm is 6 cm: $24-2t=6 \Rightarrow t=9$ hours.

Total: 35 marks

Check: gradient = rise ÷ run, with the sign · three well-spaced points for a straight line · first-named variable on the vertical axis · label axes with units.
