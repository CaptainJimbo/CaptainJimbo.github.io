Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 9 · Unit 3

Quadratics — Sequences · Expanding · Factorising · Solving quadratic equations

Exercises

No calculator needed. ★ = challenge question.

  1. For each sequence, write down the next two terms and say whether it is arithmetic, geometric or quadratic.
    a$4,\ 11,\ 18,\ 25,\ \ldots$b$3,\ 6,\ 12,\ 24,\ \ldots$c$2,\ 5,\ 10,\ 17,\ \ldots$d$81,\ 27,\ 9,\ 3,\ \ldots$

    a $32,\ 39$ — arithmetic (add 7).   b $48,\ 96$ — geometric (multiply by 2).

    c $26,\ 37$ — quadratic (differences $3,5,7,9,11$; the terms are $n^{2}+1$).   d $1,\ \tfrac13$ — geometric (multiply by $\tfrac13$).

  2. Find the $n$th term of each arithmetic sequence.
    • a $5,\ 8,\ 11,\ 14,\ \ldots$
    • b $20,\ 16,\ 12,\ 8,\ \ldots$
    • c Is 152 a term of the sequence in part a? Explain.

    a Common difference $3$: $3n+2$.   b Common difference $-4$: $24-4n$.

    c $3n+2=152$ gives $3n=150$, $n=50$. Yes — it is the 50th term.

  3. $T(n)$ is the $n$th term of a sequence.
    • a Write down the first three terms and the 10th term of $T(n)=n^{2}+4$.
    • b Write down the first three terms and the 10th term of $T(n)=2n^{2}-1$.
    • c Find the $n$th term of $4,\ 7,\ 12,\ 19,\ \ldots$
    • d Find the $n$th term of $2,\ 8,\ 18,\ 32,\ \ldots$

    a $5,\ 8,\ 13$; $T(10)=100+4=104$.   b $1,\ 7,\ 17$; $T(10)=200-1=199$.

    c Second differences are 2, so compare with $n^{2}$: $1,4,9,16$. Each term is 3 more: $n^{2}+3$.

    d Each term is twice a square number: $2n^{2}$.

  4. Expand and simplify.
    a$(x+4)(x+6)$b$(x+7)(x-3)$c$(x-5)(x-8)$d$(x-9)(x+2)$

    a $x^{2}+10x+24$   b $x^{2}+4x-21$   c $x^{2}-13x+40$   d $x^{2}-7x-18$

  5. Expand and simplify.
    a$(x+6)^{2}$b$(x-7)^{2}$c$(x+8)(x-8)$d$(2x+3)(x-5)$e$(3x-2)(2x+5)$

    a $x^{2}+12x+36$   b $x^{2}-14x+49$   c $x^{2}-64$ (the $x$ terms cancel)

    d $2x^{2}-10x+3x-15=2x^{2}-7x-15$   e $6x^{2}+15x-4x-10=6x^{2}+11x-10$

  6. Factorise.
    a$x^{2}+7x$b$x^{2}+9x+14$c$x^{2}+2x-15$d$x^{2}-10x+24$e$x^{2}-3x-28$

    a $x(x+7)$   b $(x+2)(x+7)$   c $(x+5)(x-3)$

    d $(x-4)(x-6)$   e $(x-7)(x+4)$   (find two numbers that multiply to the constant and add to the coefficient of $x$)

  7. Factorise.
    a$x^{2}-81$b$x^{2}+14x+49$c$9x^{2}-25$d$y^{2}-z^{2}$

    a $(x+9)(x-9)$   b $(x+7)^{2}$   c $(3x+5)(3x-5)$   d $(y+z)(y-z)$

  8. Solve.
    a$x^{2}+5x+6=0$b$x^{2}-x-20=0$c$x^{2}-9x=0$d$x^{2}=49$e$x^{2}+3x=10$

    a $(x+2)(x+3)=0$: $x=-2$ or $x=-3$   b $(x-5)(x+4)=0$: $x=5$ or $x=-4$   c $x(x-9)=0$: $x=0$ or $x=9$

    d $x=7$ or $x=-7$   e $x^{2}+3x-10=0$, $(x+5)(x-2)=0$: $x=-5$ or $x=2$

  9. A rectangle has sides $(x+5)$ cm and $(x-2)$ cm. Its area is 60 cm².
    • a Show that $x^{2}+3x-70=0$.
    • b Solve the equation to find $x$.
    • c Work out the perimeter of the rectangle.

    a $(x+5)(x-2)=60$, so $x^{2}+3x-10=60$, giving $x^{2}+3x-70=0$.

    b $(x+10)(x-7)=0$, so $x=7$ ($x=-10$ would give negative lengths).

    c Sides 12 cm and 5 cm; perimeter $=2(12+5)=34$ cm.

  10. A sequence has $n$th term $T(n)=2n^{2}+3$.
    • a Is 203 a term of the sequence? Explain.
    • b Is 150 a term of the sequence? Explain.

    a $2n^{2}+3=203$ gives $n^{2}=100$, $n=10$. Yes, the 10th term.

    b $2n^{2}+3=150$ gives $n^{2}=73.5$, which is not a square number, so no.

  11. A geometric sequence starts $5,\ 15,\ 45,\ \ldots$
    • a Work out the 6th term.
    • b Which is the first term greater than 10 000?

    a Multiply by 3 each time: $5,15,45,135,405,1215$. The 6th term is $1215$.

    b 7th term $3645$, 8th term $10\,935$. The 8th term is the first above $10\,000$.

Check: $(x+a)(x+b)=x^{2}+(a+b)x+ab$ · $(x+a)(x-a)=x^{2}-a^{2}$ · to solve, make one side zero, factorise, then set each bracket equal to zero · always expand your factorisation to check.
