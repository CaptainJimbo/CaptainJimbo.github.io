Algebra 4 — Using formulae · Change of subject

Exercises

Calculator allowed for the numerical parts. Marks in brackets. ★ = harder question.

  1. [6]Make $x$ the subject of each equation.
    a $d-4x=f$b $p=3(q+x)$c $\dfrac{x+a}{b}=c$

    a Add $4x$, subtract $f$: $d-f=4x \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{d-f}{4}$.

    b Divide by 3: $\dfrac{p}{3}=q+x \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{p}{3}-q$ (or $\dfrac{p-3q}{3}$).

    c Multiply by $b$: $x+a=bc \Rightarrow x=bc-a$.

  2. [9]Make $x$ the subject of each formula.
    a $x^2-B=3C$b $\dfrac{x^2}{y}-z=a$c $p(x-q)=x$

    a $x^2=3C+B \Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt{3C+B}$.

    b $\dfrac{x^2}{y}=a+z \Rightarrow x^2=y(a+z) \Rightarrow x=\pm\sqrt{y(a+z)}$.

    c $x$ appears twice — expand and collect the $x$ terms on one side: $px-pq=x \Rightarrow px-x=pq \Rightarrow x(p-1)=pq \Rightarrow x=\dfrac{pq}{p-1}$.

  3. [4]The formula $s=ut+\tfrac{1}{2}at^2$ connects distance $s$, initial speed $u$, time $t$ and acceleration $a$.
    • a Make $a$ the subject.
    • b Calculate $a$ when $s=120$ m, $u=3$ m/s and $t=6$ s.

    a $s-ut=\tfrac{1}{2}at^2 \Rightarrow a=\dfrac{2(s-ut)}{t^2}$.

    b $a=\dfrac{2(120-18)}{36}=\dfrac{204}{36}=5.67$ m/s².

  4. [6]A shape is a semicircle of radius $r$.
    • a Write a formula for its perimeter $P$ (curved edge plus diameter).
    • b Write a formula for its area $A$.
    • c Find the value of $r$ for which $A=P$ numerically.

    a Half a circumference plus the diameter: $P=\pi r+2r=r(\pi+2)$.

    b $A=\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2$.

    c $\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r^2=r(\pi+2)$. Divide by $r$: $\tfrac{1}{2}\pi r=\pi+2 \Rightarrow r=\dfrac{2(\pi+2)}{\pi}=3.27$ (3 s.f.).

  5. [3]The volume of a cylinder is $V=\pi r^2h$. A cylinder has volume 500 cm³ and height 10 cm. Find its radius.

    Rearrange: $r^2=\dfrac{V}{\pi h} \Rightarrow r=\sqrt{\dfrac{V}{\pi h}}=\sqrt{\dfrac{500}{10\pi}}=3.99$ cm (positive root only — it's a length).

  6. [4]Make $t$ the subject of $\ m=\dfrac{3t+1}{t-2}$.

    Multiply out: $m(t-2)=3t+1 \Rightarrow mt-2m=3t+1$. Collect $t$ terms: $mt-3t=2m+1 \Rightarrow t(m-3)=2m+1 \Rightarrow t=\dfrac{2m+1}{m-3}$.

Total: 32 marks

Check: undo operations in reverse order, one step at a time · a square root gives $\pm$ unless the quantity must be positive · if the new subject appears twice, collect its terms and factorise it out.
