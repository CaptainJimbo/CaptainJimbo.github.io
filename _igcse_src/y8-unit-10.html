Charts and diagrams — Tables · Averages · Pie charts · Stem and leaf · Scatter graphs

Exercises

A calculator is allowed for calc questions. Squared paper and a protractor are needed for the drawing questions. ★ = challenge question.

  1. A pie chart shows 72 birds seen in a garden one day. The sector angles are: starling $150^\circ$, pigeon $90^\circ$, goldfinch $45^\circ$, sparrow $45^\circ$, other $30^\circ$. How many of the birds were a starlings, b sparrows, c goldfinches?

    $360^\circ$ represents 72 birds, so $1^\circ$ is $72\div360=0.2$ birds. a $150\times0.2=30$  b $45\times0.2=9$  c $9$. (Pigeon 18, other 6; total 72 ✓)

  2. A survey asked people how many items they bought online in a week.
    Items012345
    Frequency58121082
    • a Work out the range.
    • b Write down the mode.
    • c calc Work out the mean, correct to 1 decimal place.
    • d Find the median.

    a $5-0=5$.  b 2 (highest frequency, 12).

    c Total items $=0\times5+1\times8+2\times12+3\times10+4\times8+5\times2=104$; people $=45$. Mean $=104\div45=2.3$.

    d 45 people → the 23rd value. Running totals: 5, 13, 25 — the 23rd is in the "2" group. Median $=2$.

  3. Draw a pie chart for the data in question 2.

    Angle $=\dfrac{\text{frequency}}{45}\times360=8^\circ$ per person: $0\to40^\circ$, $1\to64^\circ$, $2\to96^\circ$, $3\to80^\circ$, $4\to64^\circ$, $5\to16^\circ$ (sum $360^\circ$ ✓). Label each sector.

  4. The table shows what two families spent on food each week over a year.
    MeanMedianRange
    Petrou family€85€82.50€38
    Lam family€75€81€24
    • a Write two sentences comparing the families' spending.
    • b Why is there unlikely to be a modal value for a family's weekly food bill?

    a On average the Petrou family spend more (mean €85 vs €75; medians €82.50 vs €81, so the typical week is similar — the Petrou mean is pulled up by some expensive weeks). The Petrou family's spending varies more (range €38 vs €24); the Lam family are more consistent.

    b Amounts like €83.47 are rarely exactly repeated, so no value occurs more often than the others.

  5. Two pans of hot water were left to cool, one with a lid. Temperatures (°C) every 10 minutes:
    Time (min)0102030405060
    Lid off100624028222020
    Lid on100785842302420
    • a Draw both sets of data as line graphs on the same axes.
    • b What is room temperature? How do you know?
    • c Compare how long the pans took to reach room temperature.
    • d What was the difference in temperature after 30 minutes?

    a Time on the horizontal axis; two curves, both falling and levelling off.

    b $20\,^\circ$C — both graphs flatten out at that value.  c Lid off: about 50 minutes. Lid on: about 60 minutes — the lid keeps the heat in, so it cools more slowly.  d $42-28=14\,^\circ$C.

  6. The masses (g) of ten eggs: 58.5, 61.3, 55.2, 58.6, 49.1, 45.2, 64.7, 61.2, 55.0, 59.5. Copy and complete the grouped frequency table with classes $45\le m<50$, $50\le m<55$, $55\le m<60$, $60\le m<65$.

    $45\le m<50$: 49.1, 45.2 → 2. $50\le m<55$: none → 0. $55\le m<60$: 58.5, 55.2, 58.6, 55.0, 59.5 → 5. $60\le m<65$: 61.3, 64.7, 61.2 → 3. Total 10 ✓.

  7. Copy and complete the two-way table about people at a leisure centre.
    TennisHockeyBadmintonTotal
    Female201567
    Male2670
    Total4435
    • a How many people were playing hockey?
    • b How many females played hockey?

    Female hockey $=67-20-15=32$. Male tennis $=44-20=24$. Male badminton $=35-15=20$ (check: $24+26+20=70$ ✓). Hockey total $=32+26=58$. Grand total $=137$.

    a 58  b 32.

  8. A stem-and-leaf diagram shows reaction times (seconds):  10 | 5 7  ·  11 | 0 4 9  ·  12 | 6 6 8 9  ·  13 | 2 5 5 6 8  ·  14 | 1 3 3   (key: 10 | 7 means 10.7 s).
    • a What is the range?
    • b What is the median?
    • c What is the mode?

    17 values in order. a $14.3-10.5=3.8$ s.  b The 9th value: 10.5, 10.7, 11.0, 11.4, 11.9, 12.6, 12.6, 12.8, 12.9 → median $12.9$ s.  c 12.6 and 13.5 both appear twice — two modes.

  9. The table shows the wingspan and leg length of some falcons (cm).
    Wingspan102120117112132111126118121107
    Leg length40474544544550435047
    • a Draw a scatter graph (wingspan across, leg length up).
    • b Describe the correlation.
    • c Draw a line of best fit and use it to estimate the leg length of a falcon with wingspan 124 cm.

    b Positive correlation — larger wingspan goes with longer legs — fairly strong (points close to a line).

    c A line of best fit through the middle of the points, roughly from $(102,40)$ to $(132,54)$: at 124 cm it gives about $50$ cm (accept 49–51).

  10. calc Estimate the total weekly pocket money given to children in a country using these facts: 70% of 5–16 year olds get pocket money; the average is €6.50 a week; there are about 8 million children aged 5–16.

    $0.70\times8\,000\,000=5\,600\,000$ children; $5\,600\,000\times6.50\approx€36\,000\,000$ — about €36 million a week. (An estimate: the "average" and the 70% are themselves rounded.)

Check: pie chart — angle per item = 360 ÷ total · mean = total ÷ count, median = middle of the ordered list, mode = most common · compare data with an average and a measure of spread.
