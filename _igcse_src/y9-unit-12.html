Pre-IGCSE Mathematics · Year 9 · Unit 12

Probability — Sets and Venn diagrams · Sample spaces · Tree diagrams · Experimental probability

Exercises

Give probabilities as fractions in their simplest form, or as decimals. ★ = challenge question.

  1. $A$ is the set of the first six even numbers. $B$ is the set of the factors of 12.
    • a List the elements of $A$ and of $B$, using set notation.
    • b List the elements of $A\cap B$.
    • c List the elements of $A\cup B$ and write down $n(A\cup B)$.
    • d List the elements that are in $A$ but not in $B$.

    a $A=\{2,4,6,8,10,12\}$, $B=\{1,2,3,4,6,12\}$.

    b $A\cap B=\{2,4,6,12\}$ (in both).   c $A\cup B=\{1,2,3,4,6,8,10,12\}$ (in either), so $n(A\cup B)=8$.

    d $\{8,10\}$.

  2. 50 students are asked which sports they play. 28 play football (F), 19 play tennis (T), 9 play both, and the rest play neither.
    • a Draw a Venn diagram to show this information.
    • b A student is chosen at random. Work out the probability that the student plays   i football only   ii at least one of the sports   iii neither sport.

    a Football only $28-9=19$; tennis only $19-9=10$; neither $50-19-9-10=12$.

    b i $\dfrac{19}{50}$   ii $\dfrac{19+9+10}{50}=\dfrac{38}{50}=\dfrac{19}{25}$   iii $\dfrac{12}{50}=\dfrac{6}{25}$

  3. A fair spinner numbered 1 to 4 is spun and a fair coin is thrown.
    • a List all the possible outcomes.
    • b Work out the probability of getting   i a head and an even number   ii a tail and a number greater than 1.

    a $1H,\ 2H,\ 3H,\ 4H,\ 1T,\ 2T,\ 3T,\ 4T$ — 8 equally likely outcomes.

    b i $2H$ and $4H$: $\dfrac28=\dfrac14$   ii $2T,\ 3T,\ 4T$: $\dfrac38$

  4. Two fair dice are rolled and the scores are added. There are 36 equally likely outcomes. Work out the probability that
    • a the total is 9
    • b both dice show the same number
    • c the total is less than 5.

    a $(3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3)$: $\dfrac{4}{36}=\dfrac19$

    b Six doubles: $\dfrac{6}{36}=\dfrac16$

    c Totals 2, 3 and 4 come from $(1,1),(1,2),(2,1),(1,3),(2,2),(3,1)$: $\dfrac{6}{36}=\dfrac16$

  5. A bag contains only red, blue and green counters. $P(\text{red})=0.35$ and $P(\text{blue})=0.4$.
    • a Work out $P(\text{green})$.
    • b Work out $P(\text{red or green})$.
    • c A fair dice is rolled. Are the events “even number” and “prime number” mutually exclusive? Explain.

    a The probabilities add to 1: $1-0.35-0.4=0.25$.

    b The events are mutually exclusive, so add: $0.35+0.25=0.6$.

    c No — 2 is both even and prime, so the two events can happen together.

  6. The probability that Jo's bus is on time is 0.9. The probability that her train is on time is 0.7. The two events are independent. Work out the probability that
    • a both are on time
    • b the bus is on time and the train is late
    • c both are late
    • d at least one of them is late.

    a $0.9\times0.7=0.63$

    b $0.9\times0.3=0.27$

    c $0.1\times0.3=0.03$

    d $1-P(\text{both on time})=1-0.63=0.37$

  7. A bag contains 3 red counters and 2 blue counters. A counter is taken at random, its colour is noted and it is put back. A second counter is then taken.
    • a Draw a tree diagram to show all the outcomes and their probabilities.
    • b Work out the probability of getting   i two reds   ii two blues   iii one of each colour.

    b i $\dfrac{9}{25}$   ii $\dfrac{4}{25}$   iii $RB$ or $BR$: $\dfrac{6}{25}+\dfrac{6}{25}=\dfrac{12}{25}$. (Check: $\dfrac{9}{25}+\dfrac{12}{25}+\dfrac{4}{25}=1$.)

  8. A biased coin has $P(\text{head})=0.6$. It is thrown twice.
    • a Work out the probability of exactly one head.
    • b Work out the probability of at least one head.

    a $HT$ or $TH$: $0.24+0.24=0.48$

    b $1-P(TT)=1-0.16=0.84$

  9. A spinner has five equal sectors labelled A to E. It is spun 200 times.
    SectorABCDE
    Frequency3844364339
    • a Write down the theoretical probability of landing on B.
    • b Work out the experimental probability (relative frequency) of landing on B.
    • c How many times would you expect a fair spinner to land on each sector?
    • d Do the results suggest the spinner is fair? Explain.

    a $\dfrac15=0.2$   b $\dfrac{44}{200}=0.22$   c $0.2\times200=40$ times

    d Yes, probably. Every frequency is close to the expected 40 (between 36 and 44), and with 200 spins small differences like these are what chance produces.

  10. A box contains 5 red pens and 3 green pens. Two pens are taken at random without replacement. Work out the probability that
    • a both pens are red
    • b the pens are different colours.

    a After one red is taken, 4 of the remaining 7 are red: $\dfrac58\times\dfrac47=\dfrac{20}{56}=\dfrac{5}{14}$

    b Red then green, or green then red: $\dfrac58\times\dfrac37+\dfrac38\times\dfrac57=\dfrac{30}{56}=\dfrac{15}{28}$

  11. A bag contains $n$ red counters and 4 blue counters. The probability of picking a red counter is $\dfrac35$.
    • a Work out the value of $n$.
    • b A counter is picked, replaced, and a second counter is picked. Work out the probability that both are blue.

    a $\dfrac{n}{n+4}=\dfrac35$, so $5n=3n+12$ and $n=6$.

    b $P(\text{blue})=\dfrac{4}{10}=\dfrac25$. With replacement the picks are independent: $\dfrac25\times\dfrac25=\dfrac{4}{25}$.

Check: $\cap$ means “and” (both), $\cup$ means “or” (either) · mutually exclusive: add · independent: multiply · multiply along the branches of a tree, add between the outcomes you want · P(at least one) = 1 − P(none).
